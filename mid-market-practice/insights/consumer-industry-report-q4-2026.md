WHAT IT MEANS FOR OWNERS

*Buyers are paying for brands and operators that have held price and volume, that sell through more than one channel, and that have room to add distribution or locations.*

An owner who can show price increases by product or service line, with unit volume, and customer retention beside them, gives a buyer the evidence that supports a higher valuation.

### Tariffs, Fuel, and Financing: Three Costs Buyers Are Repricing

Tariffs, fuel, and interest rates moved in the past year, and the moves change what a buyer will treat as normal earnings.

A consumer company that reaches the market in the next year will present earnings that passed through three tariff regimes, a fuel shock, and the first increase in the federal funds rate since 2023. Buyers are rebuilding each of those costs period by period before they settle on the earnings they will pay for.

The effect differs from one company to the next. YETI recorded [a $45.6 million net benefit](https://sgbonline.com/yeti-boosts-fy-eps-outlook-after-q2-earnings-beat-sales-climb-9-percent/) from tariff refunds in its second quarter and Walmart said it [reinvested its refunds into price](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000104169/000010416926000145/earningsreleasefy27q2.htm), while [91% of the foodservice equipment manufacturers](https://nafem.org/2026/03/11/advocacy-issues-survey-pressure-is-the-new-normal/) surveyed by their trade association reported negative effects from tariffs and 78% said they were passing the cost on to customers.

*An owner who can hand a buyer landed cost by period, fuel recovery by contract, and a clear account of how the business is financed shortens diligence and protects the earnings figure the price is built on.*

WHAT BUYERS ARE REBUILDING

01

Tariffs

The Supreme Court held on February 20 that the International Emergency Economic Powers Act [does not authorize tariffs](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/24-1287_4gcj.pdf), a temporary 10% tariff followed, and since July 24 [Section 301 tariffs of 10% to 12.5%](https://www.wiley.law/alert-New-Forced-Labor-Tariffs-Imposed-on-60-US-Trading-Partners) apply to 60 trading partners. Section 232 duties stayed in place, including [25% on upholstered furniture](https://info.expeditors.com/newsflash/white-house-delays-increase-of-section-232-tariffs-on-furniture-and-cabinets), and duty-free treatment for parcels under $800 [remains suspended](https://www.strtrade.com/trade-news-resources/str-trade-report/trade-report/february/de-minimis-suspension-remains-in-place).

**WHAT BUYERS ASK FOR**  Landed cost under each regime, the status of any refund claim, and the sourcing alternatives the company has already qualified.

02

Fuel

On-highway diesel averaged $6.29 a gallon in September against $3.75 a year earlier ([FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/GASDESM)). The Energy Information Administration’s October outlook expects retail diesel to average [about $4.50 next year](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/), which would still be well above the price distributors and route-based service companies paid a year ago.

**WHAT BUYERS ASK FOR**  Which customer contracts carry fuel surcharges or cost escalators, how quickly they reset, and how much of the increase the company has recovered.

03

Financing

The Federal Reserve [raised its target range](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm) to 3.75% to 4.00% in September and projects a policy rate of [4.1% at the end of 2026 and 2027](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcprojtabl20260916.htm). Spreads on new direct loans to middle-market buyouts [widened by roughly 25 basis points](https://insuranceaum.com/private-credit-market-update-q2-2026) in the second quarter, and earn-outs appeared in 24% of 2025 transactions with a median equal to 34% of the closing payment ([SRS Acquiom study, as summarized by Fasken](https://www.fasken.com/en/knowledge/2026/05/private-ma-deal-trends-to-watch-key-takeaways-from-srs-acquioms-2026-study)).

**WHAT BUYERS ASK FOR**  How much of the price is paid in cash at closing, how much is contingent, and how much is rolled into the buyer’s equity.

-   OUR VIEW

Buyers are separating brands that hold volume from brands that rely on price. Hershey’s organic sales rose 3.6% on about 12 points of price against an 8-point volume decline, store brands reached 23.8% of units, and packaged food companies trade at a median 8.8x EBITDA against 9.8x a year ago. Strategics still pay for growth categories, as Hormel did at 10.7x for Brakebush Brothers.

Beauty, personal care, over-the-counter health, and pet products drew a large share of this year’s brand acquisitions, and household and personal care companies trade at a median 17.1x EBITDA against 16.3x a year ago. Dollar growth runs ahead of unit demand, with non-edible packaged goods up 3.1% in dollars and down 2.2% in units, so buyers will test unit trends and tariff exposure by product.

Foodservice distribution companies trade at a median 13.6x EBITDA, level with a year ago, and Sysco agreed to pay 14.6x operating income for Jetro Restaurant Depot. Operators are spending carefully, with the share of manufacturers’ agents reporting more quoting down to 33%, and a 314-unit Wendy’s franchisee in Chapter 11 shows where credit risk sits.

Sponsors and public acquirers keep paying for scale and recurring customers: Leonard Green paid $3.1 billion for Mister Car Wash, Oak Hill agreed to buy Guild Garage Group at a reported 16x EBITDA, and Rollins allocated about 89% of its Romex price to customer contracts and goodwill. Consumer services shares are down 33.3% in a year and the median multiple is 11.9x against 18.2x a year ago.

OUR ADVICE TO OWNERS1Show price, volume, and customer retention by product or service line, because buyers separate growth that comes from units from growth that comes from price.2

Assemble landed cost by period, fuel recovery by contract, and the financing terms of the business before a process, since buyers are rebuilding earnings from those three costs.

3

Approach operating companies and platforms in adjacent categories alongside sponsors; nine of the 17 named acquirers of our consumer clients were operating companies or platforms.

USDA ECONOMIC RESEARCH SERVICE

September 25, 2026

Food Price Outlook: Summary Findings

> *“In 2026, prices for all food are forecast to increase 2.9 percent, with a forecast interval of 2.5 to 3.2 percent.”*

USDA Economic Research Service, Food Price Outlook

**Key takeaway.** Food-at-home prices are forecast to rise 2.4% and food-away-from-home prices 3.5%, with beef and veal up 9.4% and eggs down 29.4%. Input costs are moving in different directions by category, which is why buyers ask for price and cost history by product line.

[READ HERE](https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/food-price-outlook/summary-findings)

NATIONAL RESTAURANT ASSOCIATION

September 30, 2026

Restaurant Performance Index: August 2026

> *“The National Restaurant Association’s Restaurant Performance Index (RPI) rose for the fourth consecutive month in August, largely because of improvements in both same-store sales and customer traffic levels.”*

National Restaurant Association, Restaurant Performance Index

**Key takeaway.** The index stood at 100.6, with 51% of operators reporting higher same-store sales than a year earlier and 46% still reporting lower traffic. Distributors and equipment dealers that sell to restaurants are valued partly on the health of those customers.

[READ HERE](https://restaurant.org/NRA/media/Research/RPI/2024/RPI-August-2026.pdf)

- PRIVATE LABEL MANUFACTURERS ASSOCIATION July 8, 2026

Store Brands Continue Gains in Unit Sales and Shares

> *“The disconnect between unit sales and dollar sales reflects, in part, fluctuating national brand pricing.”*

Peggy Davies, President, Private Label Manufacturers Association

**Key takeaway.** Store brand unit share reached a record 23.8% in the six months to mid-June, while store brand dollar sales were flat and national brand dollars rose 2.2%. Buyers pay for brands that hold unit volume while they raise price.

[READ HERE](https://plma.com/article/store-brands-continue-gains-unit-sales-and-shares)

**DELOITTE**

September 10, 2026

Deloitte Forecasts Holiday Retail Sales to Reach $1.70 Trillion to $1.71 Trillion

> *“As they look to get more out of their dollars, we continue to see value-seeking behaviors across income levels, including switching among brands and retailers and using promotions to manage spending.”*

Natalie Martini, vice chair, Deloitte, and US retail and consumer products leader

**Key takeaway.** Holiday sales are forecast to rise 4.0% to 4.8% and e-commerce sales 7.5% to 8.4%. Online sales growing at nearly twice the pace of the total keeps channel mix in front of every buyer of a consumer brand.

[READ HERE](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/about/press-room/deloitte-forecasts-holiday-retail-sales.html)
