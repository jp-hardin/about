Industry at a Glance

American manufacturing has expanded for nine consecutive months. Acquirers across the industrial segments are buying capabilities they cannot build in time, because skilled labor is scarce, certifications take years, and new equipment is sold out well ahead. Borrowing costs rose in September, and buyers have become more selective about what they pay for.

Market Conditions

The Quarter in Brief

Demand spans the five segments, and buyers are acquiring through existing platforms. Higher financing costs in September reduced sponsors’ borrowing capacity.

WHAT IT MEANS FOR OWNERS

Strategic acquirers and sponsor platforms are still buying machining, distribution, contracting, environmental, and logistics businesses for capabilities already in place.

They are paying the most for companies tied to power, data centers, aerospace, and service work that codes and regulations require.

Domestic manufacturing demand is broad

The ISM Manufacturing PMI was 54.5 in September, its ninth consecutive month of expansion, and orders for manufacturing technology reached $4.03 billion in the first seven months of the year, 37.1% ahead of 2025 (ISM; AMT data reported by MDM). Manufacturing capacity utilization was 75.9% in September, so the scarce inputs are skilled labor and certified capacity rather than floor space (Federal Reserve G.17).

Private equity is buying through platforms it already owns

Add-on acquisitions made up about three-quarters of all buyouts in the second quarter (PitchBook), and Pye-Barker Fire & Safety completed 22 acquisitions in the first half of the year alone (Pye-Barker). The sponsor at the table for a founder-owned industrial company is usually an operating platform that already knows its trade.

Power and data center work is the strongest source of demand

Quanta Services ended June with backlog of $53.4 billion against $35.8 billion a year earlier (Quanta Services). Public infrastructure funding is less certain, because the surface transportation authorization was extended only through December 11 under the continuing resolution signed in September (AASHTO Journal).

Recurring service revenue is valued above project revenue

APi Group is working toward 60% of revenue from inspection and service, and Transcat’s service revenue grew 27% (APi Group; Transcat). Buyers pay for service, repair, inspection, and aftermarket parts revenue when an owner reports it separately from project and product sales, with the renewal history behind it.

Financing costs more and buyers are more selective

Global M&A value fell 41% from the second quarter to $993 billion in the third, and the Federal Reserve raised its target range to 3.75% to 4.00% on September 16 (LSEG data reported by Reuters; Federal Reserve). GF Data’s average multiple eased to 7.0 times trailing adjusted EBITDA from 7.3 times, and debt on platform acquisitions fell to 2.9 times from 3.4 times (GF Data via ACG). A sponsor that can borrow half a turn less bids less or moves more of the price into earn-outs; strategic buyers are affected less.

Power Demand: How the Buildout Reaches the Industrial Segments

Electricity demand grows about 2% a year and the equipment is sold out years ahead, so acquirers buy the people, permits, and delivery slots already in place.

The Energy Information Administration expects US electricity sales to grow about 2% in 2026 and again in 2027, driven by data center development and commercial and industrial activity (EIA). NERC raised its 10-year forecast of summer peak demand growth to 224 gigawatts from 132 (POWER Magazine), and Meta alone expects capital expenditures of $130 billion to $145 billion in 2026 (Meta).

The equipment needed to meet that demand is committed years ahead. GE Vernova’s gas turbine backlog and slot reservations rose to 116 gigawatts from 100 during the second quarter (GE Vernova), and lead times for generator step-up transformers reached 160 weeks in the first quarter (Reuters, citing Wood Mackenzie). Generac’s commercial and industrial sales grew 29% and its data center backlog reached about $1.6 billion (Generac).

Power is the constraint

HOW THE DEMAND REACHES EACH SEGMENT

MANUFACTURING & INDUSTRIAL TECHNOLOGY

nVent agreed to buy Maverick Power, a Texas maker of switchgear and modular power for data centers, for $1.75 billion plus up to $550 million, which it put at 11.5 times anticipated 2026 adjusted EBITDA on the upfront payment (nVent).

INDUSTRIAL DISTRIBUTION & SERVICES

EMCOR’s US Industrial Services segment grew 25.9% in the second quarter (EMCOR). The segment serves refining, petrochemical, utility, and power customers, where turnaround and maintenance work recurs on a schedule that an acquirer can underwrite.

BUILDING PRODUCTS & CONSTRUCTION SERVICES

Contractors doing data center work reported 9.9 months of backlog against 8.3 months for those that are not (ABC). Limbach lowered its margin guidance after gross margin fell to 21.5% from 28.0%, mainly on the lower margins of an acquired business, with competition for skilled labor and materials in data center markets as a second factor (Limbach).

ENERGY, POWER & ENVIRONMENTAL SERVICES

Mission Critical Group bought a Texas switchgear manufacturer and a transformer supplier within four months of each other (Dallas Innovates; Distribution Strategy Group). CORE keeps more than 1,000 transformers in inventory and delivers custom units in as little as 16 weeks, against lead times measured in years for new production.

TRANSPORTATION & LOGISTICS

Data center construction spending is up more than 73% over the past year (ABC, reported by Construction Dive), and the transformers, switchgear, and generators behind it move by heavy haul and project freight. JK Technology Services bought 360 Rigging, a Manassas, Virginia crane and rigging company that installs equipment in data centers (Vertikal).

Our View

Our View

Five conclusions from the quarter, and what they mean for an owner planning a sale in 2027.

OUR VIEW IN ONE LINE

Buyers are paying premiums for specialized capabilities. Higher borrowing costs affect the offers they can make.

FOR AN OWNER GOING TO MARKET IN 2027

Report service, inspection, and aftermarket revenue separately from project and product sales, with renewal history.

Show backlog by end market and funded status, and name the technicians who will perform it.

Document how steel, diesel, and transformer cost increases pass through each contract.

01

Specialized capabilities command higher prices

nVent paid 11.5 times for switchgear it could not build in time, Clean Harbors about 17 times standalone EBITDA for a route network, and Redwood and Imperative bought Laredo brokers for licenses and bonded space. A founder-owned company that holds a certification, a permit, a delivery slot, or a license that takes years to replicate is priced on that asset, at 8 to 12 times, against about 6 times for project and product capacity.

02
Higher rates affect sponsor financing

With the funds rate at 3.75% to 4.00%, the 10-year near 5%, and sponsor leverage at 2.9 times, a platform bid carries half a turn less debt than it did in the spring. Strategic acquirers with cash and synergies are affected less, and we expect their share of mid-market industrial sales, 56% of our closed transactions over two years, to rise through 2027. Sponsor offers will lean on earn-outs and seller notes, and the structure deserves as much attention as the headline number.

03

Data center work supports listed contractors’ growth

Listed building products and construction companies grew revenue 22% while total construction spending fell 1.7% and manufacturing construction fell 19%. A mid-market contractor with funded data center backlog carries a premium, and one without it competes in a shrinking market for everything else. Limbach’s margin reset shows what happens when skilled labor is bid away, so buyers will underwrite the crews along with the backlog.

04
Recurring service revenue supports valuation

APi Group is steering toward 60% inspection and service revenue, Transcat’s service line grew 27%, and a project contractor like 1901 Inc. changed hands at 5.7 times. Buyers pay for revenue that renews under codes, regulations, and maintenance schedules, and they will diligence the split. An owner who reports service, repair, inspection, and parts separately, with renewal history, sells a different company from one who reports a single revenue line.

05

Freight recovery and demand for border services

Cass shipments are up 2.1% off a two-year low, transportation shares rose a median 39% in a year, and C.H. Robinson agreed to pay $5.8 billion for RXO with $300 million of synergies behind it. Diesel at $6.29 a gallon is testing surcharge pass-through, and US-Mexico trade at a record $94.8 billion a month is why logistics platforms are buying Laredo brokers. Customs licensing, bonded warehousing, and cross-border capability will be priced above asset-based capacity through 2027.

Views are those of the Benchmark International Middle Market team as of October 19, 2026 and draw on the sources cited in The Quarter in Brief and Power Demand: How the Buildout Reaches the Industrial Segments, and 25. They are opinions, and the price of any company depends on that business.

THREE STEPS WE ADVISE OWNERS TO TAKE

1. Compare bids on proceeds

Sponsor platform leverage fell to 2.9x EBITDA from 3.4x, so owners should compare offers on cash at close, earn-out, and seller note terms rather than headline multiple alone.

2. Invite strategic buyers

Strategic acquirers account for 56% of the transactions in Recent Transactions and Our Transactions, so a process should include them alongside sponsor platforms.

3. Document licenses and delivery slots

Licenses, certifications, permits, and delivery slots belong in the first management presentation, because buyers have paid 8x to 12x for them against about 6x for businesses without them.

Featured Deal: The Carmi Companies

A Laredo logistics and customs brokerage group and a Mexico manufacturing services company have combined to form an end-to-end cross-border platform.

BENCHMARK INTERNATIONAL ADVISED

ON ITS TRANSACTION WITH

Transportation & Logistics

Cross-border freight, customs brokerage, and warehousing

READ THE ANNOUNCEMENT

THE CLIENT

The Carmi Companies

Founded in 1983 in Laredo, Texas, The Carmi Companies is a single-source logistics group that coordinates land, air, and ocean freight and provides customs brokerage, warehousing, and transloading.

THE COUNTERPARTY

The Nearshore Company

Founded in 1992 in Brownsville, Texas, The Nearshore Company offers manufacturing facilities in Mexico together with employer-of-record services.

THE RATIONALE

An end-to-end cross-border platform

The combination pairs The Nearshore Company’s Mexico manufacturing facilities and employer-of-record services with Carmi’s logistics to form an end-to-end cross-border platform.

THE MARKET

Buyers are paying for border capability

Trade with Mexico reached a monthly record of $94.8 billion in July, 27.5% above a year earlier, and $36.95 billion of it moved through Laredo (Census data reported by FreightWaves). Logistics platforms have responded by buying brokers at the border, and Redwood Logistics and Imperative Logistics Group each acquired a Laredo customs broker this year (FreightWaves on EELCO; FreightWaves on Jose D. Gonzalez, CHB).

Carmi’s brokerage licenses, bonded warehousing, and four decades of Laredo relationships are the capability. The trucks are the capacity, and buyers can rent those.

Industrials M&A, Q4 2026: Common Questions

What is happening in industrial M&A in Q4 2026?

Order books are full and financing costs more. Buyers are paying the most for what they cannot build in time: power and data center work, certified aerospace machining, and the service revenue that codes and regulations require.

What is the Middle Market team's view of industrial M&A for Q4 2026?

Buyers are paying premiums for specialized capabilities.

What are buyers paying for industrial companies?

Public acquirers disclosed prices of about 6 to 10 times EBITDA, and sponsor-owned platforms bought family-owned companies on undisclosed terms.

How are public industrial companies valued?

Distribution companies trade above any year-end multiple of the past five years; manufacturing and energy have come off their 2025 levels.

How are listed industrial companies performing?

Listed contractors grew revenue about 22% while total construction spending fell. Manufacturers reported the highest margins.
