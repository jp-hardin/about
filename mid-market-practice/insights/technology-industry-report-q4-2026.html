Market Conditions

Key Trends

Six developments are shaping demand, pricing, and deal activity as the fourth quarter begins.

AI infrastructure sets the pace of demand

+62.5%

Gartner expects IT spending of $6.37 trillion in 2026, up 14.2%, with data center systems up 62.5% and IT services up 5.3% (Gartner). Cloud infrastructure revenue grew 43% in the second quarter (Synergy Research Group).

Celestica, TD SYNNEX, and Wesco each reported double-digit growth on data center demand in the latest quarter (page 22).

Consolidators keep buying recurring revenue

Buyers announced 64 acquisitions of managed service providers in the first quarter, 73% more than a year earlier, and outside investors led 80% of them. Evergreen Services Group alone completed 47 acquisitions in 2025 (Omdia).

Evergreen, Thrive, Integris, and The 20 MSP are each absorbing several providers a year, and contracted managed revenue is what they underwrite.

Financing became more expensive in the third quarter

5.24%

The Federal Reserve raised its target range to 3.75% to 4.00% on September 16 (Federal Reserve) and the 10-year Treasury yielded 5.24% on October 1 (US Treasury). Platform acquisition debt fell to 2.9x EBITDA from 3.4x (GF Data via ACG).

At 2.9x debt and a 7.0x average price, sponsors are funding about 60% of a purchase with equity, which favors sellers with contracted revenue.

Security budgets grow while the defense mandate pauses

$240B

Gartner's July 2025 forecast puts 2026 security spending at $240 billion, up 12.5% (Gartner); cybercrime losses reached $20.9 billion in 2025 (FBI). The Department of War suspended CMMC third-party certification on July 13 (Department of War CIO).

The review closed September 11 and its report is unpublished, so a Level 2 certificate signals readiness without a contract requirement behind it.

Hardware order books are filling faster than factories ship

1.39

North American EMS bookings rose 53.5% in August while shipments rose 1.0%, a three-month book-to-bill ratio of 1.39 (Global Electronics Association). Section 301 duties of 10% to 12.5% took effect July 24 as the Section 122 duty expired (Wiley Rein).

Component prices are up 26.7% in a year (page 23), so backlog booked at 2025 prices needs pass-through terms to hold its margin.

Federal technology budgets rise as award timing slips

$75.7B

The fiscal 2027 request restores civilian agency IT to $75.7 billion after a cut to $67.9 billion in fiscal 2026 (FedScoop). A continuing resolution signed September 2 runs through December 11 and delays new program starts (SpacePolicyOnline).

Defense-weighted CACI grew 17.6% while civilian-weighted Booz Allen fell 4.2% (page 22); contract mix sets the price.

WHAT IT MEANS
FOR OWNERS

Buyers pay most readily for contracted, recurring revenue and for capability that customers or regulators require. Completed middle-market transactions averaged 7.0x EBITDA in the second quarter (GF Data via ACG), and 87% of sales above $5 million drew three or more offers (IBBA and M&A Source).

Notes

Figures are the latest published as of October 14, 2026. EMS is electronics manufacturing services. CMMC is the Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification for defense suppliers.

Tariffs, Copper, and Financing: Costs Buyers Are Repricing

Tariffs, component and copper prices and interest rates have each moved in the past year and change what buyers treat as normal earnings.

A technology company that reaches the market in the next year will present earnings that passed through three tariff regimes, a 27% rise in component prices, a record copper price, and the first increase in the federal funds rate since 2023. Buyers are rebuilding each of those costs period by period before they settle on the earnings they will pay for.

The effect differs by business. Manufacturers and distributors that import components carry duty exposure, integrators that fix a price on cabling and power work carry copper exposure, and managed service firms with contracted revenue carry mostly financing exposure.

An owner who can show landed cost by period, copper recovery by contract, and a clear account of how the business is financed shortens diligence and protects the earnings figure the price is built on.

WHAT BUYERS ARE REBUILDING

Tariffs and components

The Supreme Court held on February 20 that the International Emergency Economic Powers Act does not authorize tariffs, a 10% Section 122 tariff ran until it expired on July 24, and Section 301 tariffs of 10% to 12.5% took effect that day. A 25% Section 232 tariff applies to a narrow set of advanced chips, Section 301 duties on Chinese semiconductors remain at 50%, and component prices rose 26.7% in the year to August (Bureau of Labor Statistics via FRED).

WHAT BUYERS ASK FOR A bill of materials by country of origin with duties paid since February 2025, the status of any refund claim, and the contract terms that pass tariffs through to customers.

Copper and power

Copper averaged $13,543 a ton in July, up 38.6% from a year earlier, and reached a record $14,875 a ton on the London Metal Exchange on September 10. A 50% Section 232 tariff applies to semi-finished copper products, and Cushman & Wakefield reports that data center construction costs per megawatt are up 21% since late 2024, with switchgear up 60% and some equipment lead times beyond two years.

WHAT BUYERS ASK FOR Contracted and spot pricing for copper-bearing cable and switchgear on backlog projects, the escalation clauses that apply, and the margin effect of a 10% move in copper.

Financing

The Federal Reserve raised its target range to 3.75% to 4.00% on September 16, and the 10-year Treasury yielded 5.24% on October 1. Total debt on middle-market platform buyouts fell to 2.9x EBITDA from 3.4x in the second quarter against an average price of 7.0x (GF Data via ACG) and earn-outs appeared in 24% of 2025 transactions with a median equal to 34% of the closing payment (SRS Acquiom, as summarized by Fasken).

WHAT BUYERS ASK FOR How much of the price is paid in cash at closing, how much is contingent, and how much is rolled into the buyer’s equity, and how the company’s own debt is structured.

TO OWNERS

Document contracted revenue by customer, term, and renewal history; equity-funded buyers price what they can verify.
