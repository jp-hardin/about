- **What it means for owners.** Strategic acquirers and sponsor platforms are still buying machining, distribution, contracting, environmental, and logistics businesses for capabilities already in place.
- They are paying the most for companies tied to power, data centers, aerospace, and service work that codes and regulations require.
- **Domestic manufacturing demand is broad.** The ISM Manufacturing PMI was 54.5 in September, its ninth consecutive month of expansion, and orders for manufacturing technology reached $4.03 billion in the first seven months of the year, 37.1% ahead of 2025 (ISM; AMT data reported by MDM).
- **Private equity is buying through platforms it already owns.** Add-on acquisitions made up about three-quarters of all buyouts in the second quarter (PitchBook), and Pye-Barker Fire & Safety completed 22 acquisitions in the first half of the year alone (Pye-Barker).
- **Power and data center work is the strongest source of demand.** Quanta Services ended June with backlog of $53.4 billion against $35.8 billion a year earlier (Quanta Services).
- **Recurring service revenue is valued above project revenue.** APi Group is working toward 60% of revenue from inspection and service, and Transcat’s service revenue grew 27% (APi Group; Transcat).
- **Financing costs more and buyers are more selective.** Global M&A value fell 41% from the second quarter to $993 billion in the third, and the Federal Reserve raised its target range to 3.75% to 4.00% on September 16 (LSEG data reported by Reuters; Federal Reserve).

### Power Demand: How the Buildout Reaches the Industrial Sectors

Electricity demand grows about 2% a year and the equipment is sold out years ahead, so acquirers buy the people, permits, and delivery slots already in place.

The Energy Information Administration expects US electricity sales to grow about 2% in 2026 and again in 2027, driven by data center development and commercial and industrial activity (EIA).

The equipment needed to meet that demand is committed years ahead.

- **Manufacturing & industrial technology.** nVent agreed to buy Maverick Power, a Texas maker of switchgear and modular power for data centers, for $1.75 billion plus up to $550 million, which it put at 11.5 times anticipated 2026 adjusted EBITDA on the upfront payment (nVent).
- **Industrial distribution & services.** EMCOR’s US Industrial Services segment grew 25.9% in the second quarter (EMCOR). The segment serves refining, petrochemical, utility, and power customers, where turnaround and maintenance work recurs on a schedule that an acquirer can underwrite.
- **Building products & construction services.** Contractors doing data center work reported 9.9 months of backlog against 8.3 months for those that are not (ABC).
- **Energy, power & environmental services.** Mission Critical Group bought a Texas switchgear manufacturer and a transformer supplier within four months of each other (Dallas Innovates; Distribution Strategy Group).
- **Transportation & logistics.** Data center construction spending is up more than 73% over the past year (ABC, reported by Construction Dive), and the transformers, switchgear, and generators behind it move by heavy haul and project freight.
- **The buildout in four figures.** A buyer will ask how much of the backlog is tied to funded power and data center work, and whether the company has the technicians to perform it at the margins it has earned so far.

## 3. Our View

### Our View

Five conclusions from the quarter, and what they mean for an owner planning a sale in 2027.

- **Our view in one line.** Buyers are paying premiums for specialized capabilities. Higher borrowing costs affect the offers they can make.
- **For an owner going to market in 2027.** Report service, inspection, and aftermarket revenue separately from project and product sales, with renewal history.
- Show backlog by end market and funded status, and name the technicians who will perform it.
- Document how steel, diesel, and transformer cost increases pass through each contract.
- **Specialized capabilities command higher prices.** nVent paid 11.5 times for switchgear it could not build in time, Clean Harbors about 17 times standalone EBITDA for a route network, and Redwood and Imperative bought Laredo brokers for licenses and bonded space.
- **Higher rates affect sponsor financing.** With the funds rate at 3.75% to 4.00%, the 10-year near 5%, and sponsor leverage at 2.9 times, a platform bid carries half a turn less debt than it did in the spring.
- **Data center work supports listed contractors’ growth.** Listed building products and construction companies grew revenue 22% while total construction spending fell 1.7% and manufacturing construction fell 19%. A mid-market contractor with funded data center backlog carries a premium, and one without it competes in a shrinking market for everything else.

### Our View: Sector Outlook

Demand for owner-operated businesses differs across the five sectors.

- **Buyer demand by sector.** Ratings weigh listed-company growth, valuation, and cash conversion against Benchmark closings in the sector, on a scale of one to five.
- **Energy, Power & Environmental Strongest.** Transformer lead times above 160 weeks and 21.2% EBITDA growth, offset by 2.5x leverage.
- **Building & Construction Strong.** EBITDA up 28% on 21.8% revenue growth, and 45 Benchmark closings.
- **Manufacturing & Industrial Tech Strong.** The highest margin at 22.1% and the highest multiple at 17.3x.
- **Distribution & Services Steady.** 17.0x against 13.1x at year-end 2025, with margins near 11%.
- **Transportation & Logistics Mixed.** Lowest multiple at 12.2x and EBITDA down 0.8%, with shares up 38.9%.
- **1. Compare bids on proceeds.** Sponsor platform leverage fell to 2.9x EBITDA from 3.4x, so owners should compare offers on cash at close, earn-out, and seller note terms rather than headline multiple alone.

## 5. Deals and Buyers

### Featured Deal: The Carmi Companies

A Laredo logistics and customs brokerage group and a Mexico manufacturing services company have combined to form an end-to-end cross-border platform.

- **The Carmi Companies.** Founded in 1983 in Laredo, Texas, The Carmi Companies is a single-source logistics group that coordinates land, air, and ocean freight and provides customs brokerage, warehousing, and transloading.
- **The Nearshore Company.** Founded in 1992 in Brownsville, Texas, The Nearshore Company offers manufacturing facilities in Mexico together with employer-of-record services.
- **An end-to-end cross-border platform.** The combination pairs The Nearshore Company’s Mexico manufacturing facilities and employer-of-record services with Carmi’s logistics to form an end-to-end cross-border platform.
- **Buyers are paying for border capability.** Trade with Mexico reached a monthly record of $94.8 billion in July, 27.5% above a year earlier, and $36.95 billion of it moved through Laredo (Census data reported by FreightWaves).
- Carmi’s brokerage licenses, bonded warehousing, and four decades of Laredo relationships are the capability. The trucks are the capacity, and buyers can rent those.

## 8. What We're Reading

Four pieces from the quarter that shape how buyers and owners are talking.

- **Reading list.** The four pieces cover manufacturing orders, grid equipment supply, middle-market financing terms, and cross-border freight volume, which are separate inputs to a sale process.
- The takeaways under the quotes tie the reading to what owners should have prepared before they go to market.
- **Manufacturing PMI® at 54.5%; September 2026 ISM® Manufacturing PMI® Report.** The September PMI reached 54.5%, and new orders have grown nine months running after four months of contraction, so an owner planning a sale should show two or three quarters of order growth in the monthly financials before going to market.
- **US power companies scramble to secure equipment as surging data center demand strains supplies.** Generator step-up transformer lead times passed 160 weeks against 143 weeks on average in 2024, and utilities now buy equipment for projects they expect five years ahead, so owners serving grid and data center work should document backlog, supplier agreements, and delivery slots before launching a process.
- **GF Data Reports Show Steady Middle-Market Deal Flow Amid More Selective Financing Conditions in Q2.** Average purchase multiples slipped to 7.0x from 7.3x across 85 completed deals in the second quarter while senior debt pricing rose to 7.8%, so sellers should expect price discipline and use a quality of earnings report to defend a premium.
- **Mexico remains top US trading partner as August commerce hits $94.3B.** August trade between the US and Mexico reached $94.3 billion and Laredo two-way trade rose 28.5%, so owners with cross-border exposure should document customer mix, tariff pass-through terms, and lane concentration, which buyers test in diligence.
