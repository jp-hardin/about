Market Conditions

State of Play

Strategic buyers are setting the pace in 2026 while private equity completes fewer healthcare services transactions.

M&A market dynamics

Private equity completed an estimated 337 healthcare services transactions in the first half, a pace of 674 for the year and the lowest annual count since 2017 (PitchBook via Fierce Healthcare).

Strategic buyers carried more of the activity: medtech deal value reached $36.5 billion in the first half (PwC via MedTech Dive), and hospitals and health systems announced 56 transactions through September, 16 of them in the third quarter (Kaufman Hall).

Biopharma deal value reached $130 billion in six months, almost matching the total for all of 2025 (IQVIA).

Reimbursement and policy

Medicare has proposed 2027 physician conversion factors of $33.17 and $32.84, reductions of 1.19% and 1.68%, with the final rule pending (CMS).

Hospital outpatient, surgery center, and home health rates are proposed to rise 2.4% (CMS outpatient rule; CMS home health), and Medicare Advantage rates rise 2.48% (CMS rate notice).

Medicaid work requirements and six-month eligibility reviews for expansion adults begin in January 2027 (CMS).

Financing and macro backdrop

The Federal Reserve raised its target range to 3.75% to 4.00% on September 16 (Federal Reserve), and the 10-year Treasury yielded 5.09% on October 16 (US Treasury).

In a small sample of recent buyout loans, PitchBook found direct-lender spreads averaging 509 basis points in the second quarter against 474 in the first (PitchBook).

Completed middle-market transactions across industries averaged 7.0x EBITDA, with senior debt priced at 7.8% (GF Data via ACG).

FOR OWNERS

Medicaid redeterminations begin in January, so a Medicaid-weighted provider should expect diligence on enrollment attrition. Negotiated Medicare drug prices cut Guardian Pharmacy’s growth to 2% in one quarter. Operating companies are winning auctions against sponsors because senior debt prices at 7.8%.

Private equity paid a median 13.6x EBITDA for healthcare services companies in 2025, on targets with median revenue of $162.5 million (PitchBook via Becker’s ASC Review), and 87% of sales above $5 million across industries drew three or more offers (IBBA and M&A Source).

Demand fundamentals

National health spending grew 6.8% in the year to July 2026 and represented 18.4% of GDP (Altarum).

CMS projects nearly $9.0 trillion of spending in 2034, or 20.6% of GDP, on average annual growth of 5.4% (CMS).

Healthcare employers added 17,000 jobs in September against a twelve-month average of 33,000 (Bureau of Labor Statistics).

Trade and deal regulation

A 100% default tariff applies to covered patented pharmaceuticals, with country and company exceptions and generics excluded (White House proclamation). The medical device investigation remained open in September with no tariff imposed (iData Research).

California now requires transaction notices from private equity groups and management services organizations (Nixon Peabody), and Maine’s 180-day notice and approval regime begins January 1, 2027 (Foley & Lardner).

Figures are as of October 16, 2026, and deal counts and values are as reported by the named publisher and are revised as transactions are confirmed.

Market Fundamentals

Employment, capital investment, and drug approvals have grown since 2021, which supports demand across the four sectors.

Healthcare employed 18.55 million people in September 2026, 16.1% more than in September 2021 and 2.0% more than a year earlier.

Health care and social assistance had 1,359,000 open positions in August (Bureau of Labor Statistics JOLTS). Buyers test clinician turnover as closely as payer mix.

Three years of decline

Strategic buyers lead

Our View

Strategic buyers hold the advantage into 2027, and the owners who do best will have answered the policy questions before a buyer asks them.

THE HOUSE VIEW

Operating companies and the largest platforms are buying through 7.8% senior debt and 500-basis-point spreads because they pay from cash flow, and hospital transactions are tracking to their most active year since 2020 while private equity’s healthcare services count is at a 2017 pace.

We expect that division to hold through the first half of 2027. Sponsor volume returns once the September rate increase is absorbed and the 2027 Medicare and Medicaid rules are final, and the 2021 vintage of platforms still waiting for an exit will bring supply to market before then.

For an owner, the next twelve months favor a process built around strategic buyers, launched with the payer-mix and enrollment analysis already done and timed ahead of the sponsor exits that will crowd the second half of 2027.

01
Strategic Buyers

Operating companies bought seven of the nine named sellers in Benchmark’s last 24 months of healthcare closings, HCA has added about 70 urgent care centers this year, and Danaher, Merck KGaA, and Labcorp are paying for consumables and testing volume. A buyer list that leads with strategics and brings in sponsors for competitive tension suits a 2027 process.

02
Reimbursement Dates

The 2027 physician fee schedule proposes conversion factor cuts of 1.2% to 1.7%, Medicaid work requirements, and six-month redeterminations start in January, and laboratory fee cuts of up to 15% a year resume unless the RESULTS Act passes. Buyers will model these items line by line, so an owner who brings that analysis to the first meeting stays in control of the valuation conversation.

03
Adjusted Multiples

On company-reported adjusted EBITDA the listed reference set trades at 14.3x, with provider services at 14.2x, medical products at 10.7x and pharmacy services at 15.0x, all within about a turn of a year ago, while life sciences and diagnostics re-rated to 18.7x. Private healthcare services deals cleared a 13.6x median in 2025 on targets with $162.5 million of revenue, against 5.8x for $5 million to $50 million businesses across industries. Scale, contracted revenue, and payer mix account for the gap between those two numbers.

04
Supply

PitchBook’s healthcare analyst expects mean reversion from the 2026 low, and AMN and Thermo Fisher both described fuller pipelines on their second-quarter calls. Sponsor exits are running 26% below last year’s pace, which builds a backlog of platforms waiting to sell. Owners who launch in the first half of 2027 meet buyers before that backlog clears.

Sector by Sector

Buyers and drivers differ by sector, and this page covers providers, laboratories, and research services.

PROVIDER SERVICES

Health systems are buying access points and sponsors are trading platforms.

SUBSECTORS WE COVER

Urgent care, Optometry & eye care, Pediatric & physical therapy, Multi-specialty medical groups, Emergency medical services, Renal care

KEY TRENDS

The 100 largest urgent care operators hold 6,056 of the country’s 14,655 centers, and 55.9% of those Top 100 locations are affiliated with a hospital (Journal of Urgent Care Medicine).

HCA Healthcare acquired about 70 urgent care centers in three 2026 transactions: CommunityMed, Urgent Care Group, and Texas MedClinic (Becker’s ASC Review; Healthcare Dive).

Care in the home drew some of the largest sponsor-led provider transactions of the year, including General Atlantic’s purchase of TEAM Services Group at about $3 billion (LevinPro) and Kinderhook’s agreement to take Enhabit private at about $1.1 billion (Enhabit).

ACTIVE BUYERS

HCA Healthcare, MyEyeDr., U.S. Physical Therapy, Addus HomeCare, Concentra, Kinderhook, and Universal Health Services

LISTED REFERENCE COMPANIES

Independent practice continues to shrink, and 42.2% of physicians work in physician-owned practices against 60.1% in 2012 (AMA 2024 Benchmark Survey via Medical Economics).

LIFE SCIENCES & DIAGNOSTICS

Research spending has turned up and laboratories are selling to the national networks.

SUBSECTORS WE COVER

Clinical & pathology labs, Biologics & reagents, Clinical research sites, Human-factors & device design

KEY TRENDS

Quest grew revenue 10.2% in the second quarter on 13.1% more requisitions (Quest Diagnostics), and Labcorp agreed acquisitions with total consideration of $528.6 million in the first half (Labcorp Form 10-Q).

Medicare laboratory fee cuts of up to 15% a year could resume in 2027 unless Congress passes the RESULTS Act (Association for Diagnostics & Laboratory Medicine).

Outsourced research has recovered, with IQVIA’s net bookings up 19% (IQVIA) and Medpace growing revenue 17.2% (Medpace).

Sponsor-backed networks continue to buy clinical research sites, including Headlands Research in California and Summit Clinical Research in Dallas (Headlands Research; Summit Clinical Research).

ACTIVE BUYERS

Labcorp and Quest for laboratories; Thermo Fisher, Danaher, Agilent, and Merck KGaA for tools and reagents; Headlands Research and Summit Clinical Research for sites

LISTED REFERENCE COMPANIES

Buyers and drivers differ by sector, and this page covers products, distribution, pharmacy, and staffing.

MEDICAL PRODUCTS & DISTRIBUTION

Procedure demand is firm and consumables are outgrowing capital equipment.

SUBSECTORS WE COVER

Surgical instrument reprocessing, Emergency medical supplies, Respiratory & anesthesia products, Dental devices, Orthopedic products

KEY TRENDS

Stryker grew 9.0% organically in the second quarter (Stryker), and Steris grew healthcare-segment consumables 9% and capital equipment 1% (Steris).

Among hospital and health system executives surveyed, 41% plan to cut capital spending in 2026 (Sage Growth Partners via HFMA). Recurring supplies hold up better than capital equipment in that budget.

Distribution assets changed hands: Platinum Equity agreed in October 2025 to buy Owens & Minor’s products segment (Owens & Minor), and Apollo bought a 13% interest in McKesson’s medical-surgical unit for $1.25 billion ahead of its planned separation (McKesson).

Tariffs have moved earnings in both directions since the Supreme Court struck down the emergency-powers tariffs on February 20 (Learning Resources v. Trump).

ACTIVE BUYERS

KKR, Danaher, Stryker, Mitsui Chemicals, Cardinal Health, Aspen Surgical, Platinum Equity, and Apollo

LISTED REFERENCE COMPANIES

PHARMACY & HEALTHCARE SERVICES

Drug pricing reform is reshaping pharmacy while staffing returns to growth.

SUBSECTORS WE COVER

Specialty & mail-order pharmacy, Home infusion, Pharmaceutical delivery, Clinical staffing

KEY TRENDS

US net medicine spending reached $606 billion in 2025, up 10.6% (IQVIA Institute), while independent pharmacies reported their lowest gross profit in ten years in 2024 (NCPA 2025 Digest).

Negotiated Medicare prices have cut deepest in long-term care pharmacy, where Guardian grew revenue 2% while serving 8% more residents (Guardian Pharmacy Services).

Specialty and infusion are growing fastest, and BrightSpring’s pharmacy solutions segment grew revenue 22% to $3.4 billion in the second quarter (BrightSpring).

Healthcare staffing is forecast to grow 1% to $39.0 billion in 2026 after three years of contraction (Staffing Industry Analysts).

ACTIVE BUYERS

Guardian Pharmacy Services, Cencora, Cardinal Health, and McKesson; Knox Lane, JLL Partners, and VeloSource in staffing and logistics; Strata Critical Medical in perfusion
