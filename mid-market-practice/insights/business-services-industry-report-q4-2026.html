01

Market Conditions

Six developments across the five sectors, fourth quarter 2026

Fire and life safety consolidation

Pye-Barker Fire & Safety completed 22 acquisitions in the first half of 2026 (Pye-Barker) after 57 in 2025 (Pye-Barker, March 17). APi Group grew 10.1% organically in the second quarter and reported a backlog above $5 billion (APi Group).

Acquirers compete most actively for contracted inspection and service work.

10-year Treasury yield

4.99%

The Federal Reserve raised its target range to 3.75% to 4.00% on September 16 (Federal Reserve), and the 10-year Treasury yield averaged 4.99% in September (FRED). Average total debt on platform acquisitions fell to 2.9x EBITDA from 3.4x (GF Data via ACG).

Costlier debt puts a premium on documented earnings and contracted customers.

Engineering backlog and staffing

88%

The median engineering firm holds 12 months of backlog, and 88% of firms report at least one open position (ACEC). Bernhard Capital agreed to buy Bowman for about $1.0 billion of enterprise value, a 58% premium (Bowman).

Buyers of engineering firms are paying for licensed staff and funded backlog.

Commercial insurance pricing

−2%

Commercial premiums fell 2% on average in the second quarter, with property down 6.3% (Insurance Journal). Aon agreed on August 31 to buy USI from KKR for $17 billion including debt (Insurance Journal, September 21).

Small commercial and casualty accounts are seeing the least pricing pressure.

Staffing revenue recovery

+5%

Median staffing revenue grew 5% in August, the fastest growth since late 2022 (Staffing Industry Analysts). Temporary help employment remains 21% below its March 2022 peak (BLS via FRED).

Skilled trades, engineering, and healthcare placements are drawing the acquirers.

Advertising demand and AI

+9.6%

US advertising revenue is forecast to grow 9.6% in 2026 (MediaPost composite). Marketing budgets hold at 7.8% of company revenue, with 15.3% of those budgets directed to AI (Gartner via Marketing Dive).

Retained clients, first-party data, and measurable outcomes hold their value.

What it means

For owners

Buyers pay most readily for revenue that repeats under contract. Completed middle-market transactions averaged 7.0x EBITDA in the second quarter (GF Data via ACG), the average multiple on sales of $5 million to $50 million reached 5.8x (IBBA and M&A Source), and 87% of sales above $5 million drew three or more offers (IBBA and M&A Source release).

Notes

Figures as of October 7, 2026. The 10-year Treasury figure is the monthly average for September 2026. GF Data figures are averages on trailing adjusted EBITDA. EBITDA is earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization.

02

Our View

Expectations for the fourth quarter and the first half of 2027

Our view

The lower middle market has decoupled from the headline deal market.

Global M&A fell 41% from the second quarter on the rate move, but the decline sits in the largest transactions. Three-quarters of buyouts are add-ons financed off platform facilities and sponsor equity committed before the Fed hike, and the sub-$50 million market printed its highest multiple since early 2022 with three or more offers on 87% of sales above $5 million. We expect that spread to persist through the first half of 2027: GF Data's averages drift toward the mid-6x range as leverage keeps compressing, while business services transactions under $50 million hold between 5.5x and 6.0x, because the buyers are consolidators pricing route density and contract renewals rather than the cost of debt.

Federal funds target after the September 16 increase

87%

Structure before price

With platform leverage at 2.9x and senior debt priced at 7.8%, buyers close the gap with rollover equity, earnouts, and seller notes rather than lower headline numbers. Cash at close, now 83% to 92%, moves toward the low end of that range in 2027, so the owners who do best decide early how much deferred consideration they will accept and on what metric.

Timing

The Fed raised rates into a labor market adding 66,000 jobs a month, with unemployment at 4.2% and engineering executives putting recession odds at 41%. An owner with a renewal book or funded backlog has a better story in the first quarter of 2027 than after a softer year shows up in trailing figures, which argues for preparing now and going to market early in the new year.

By sector

Commercial & facility

Fire and life safety stays the most contested corner of business services, and the premium narrows to inspection-led, contracted revenue with company-held licenses, which is what Pye-Barker, APi, and AI Fire are paying for.

Marketing, sales & tech-enabled

The 9.6% advertising forecast masks a repricing inside agencies. Buyers pay for retained vertical specialists, proprietary data, and measurable outcomes, and labor-hour billing models see multiple compression as AI takes the production work.

Engineering, architecture & consulting

Bernhard taking Bowman private at roughly 22x reported EBITDA and a 58% premium while the listed group trades at 15x shows private capital valuing licensed staff and a year of backlog well above the public market. Founder-owned firms with power, water, and data center backlog have an open window; federally exposed firms do not.

Financial & insurance

Softening premiums slow organic growth, so the aggregators lean harder on acquisitions and Aon's purchase of USI restarts large-cap consolidation. Agency prices hold, with more of the consideration tied to retention.

Staffing & human capital

The recovery is concentrated in engineering, industrial, and healthcare placements, while temporary help employment sits 21% below its 2022 peak, so specialty firms should prepare to sell into improving comparables in 2027 and generalist commercial staffing should wait.

Notes

Expectations are Benchmark International's judgments, drawn from the data in this report, and the multiple ranges and the cash-at-close trend do not come from published figures.

Sector by Sector

Five Sectors

Buyers, drivers, and valuation tests by sector

Commercial & facility services

Contracted, inspection-driven revenue is drawing the most active consolidators.

Market size and growth

Recurring monthly revenue at the 100 largest security dealers rose 7% to $740 million in 2025 (SDM), and SGS grew 5.6% organically in the first half (SGS).

Active buyers

Pye-Barker, APi Group, AI Fire, and Cintas in fire and safety; Allied Universal in guarding; DEX Imaging and UBEO in office technology; SGS and Phenna Group in testing.

What buyers reward

Inspection, monitoring, and service contracts that renew each year, and licenses held by the company.

Marketing, sales & tech-enabled services

Advertising demand is strong, and specialists with data or a vertical focus are in demand.

Market size and growth

US internet advertising revenue reached $294.6 billion in 2025, up 13.9% (IAB and PwC), and North American promotional products sales reached a record $27.7 billion (ASI).

Active buyers

Sponsor-backed agency platforms such as OneMagnify, Power Digital, and Wpromote; Publicis and Havas; Accenture and Cognizant; HALO and Proforma in promotional products.

What buyers reward

Retained client relationships, depth in one vertical, proprietary data, and a team beyond the founder.

Engineering, architecture & consulting

Backlogs run a year deep, and private capital is paying for licensed talent.

Market size and growth

The 500 largest design firms reported $158.7 billion of revenue, up 7.4% (ENR), and 447 transactions were announced among AEC firms in 2025 (PSMJ).

Active buyers

Bernhard Capital Partners; Ardurra, owned by Littlejohn & Co.; Salas O'Brien; SAM Companies; Pape-Dawson; J.S. Held in forensic engineering.

What buyers reward

Funded backlog of a year or more, licensed staff, and a second generation of principals.

Financial & insurance services

Softer premiums are slowing organic growth while the largest brokers keep buying.

Market size and growth

Managing general agents wrote $108.7 billion of direct premium in 2025, up 17.8% (AM Best via Risk & Insurance), and advisory firm sales moved $342.9 billion of client assets in the first half (WealthManagement.com).

Active buyers

Aon, Gallagher, Inszone, EPIC, King Risk Partners, and World Insurance Associates in brokerage; Hightower and Mercer Advisors in wealth.

What buyers reward

Recurring commissions and fees with high retention, and producers under contract beyond the owner.

Staffing & human capital

Staffing revenue growth is the fastest since late 2022, led by skilled work.

Market size and growth

US temporary and contract staffing sales were $113.5 billion in 2025, down 8.5% (ASA), and industrial staffing revenue is now forecast to grow 7% in 2026 (Staffing Industry Analysts).

Active buyers

Korn Ferry, Aerotek, ALKU, CHG Healthcare, and Airswift, with sponsor-backed specialists in skilled trades and engineering.

What buyers reward

Specialty placements, gross margin held through the downturn, and direct client relationships.

Across the five sectors

Five tests buyers apply

Revenue that repeats under contract

Service agreements, retainers, and renewals.

Licensed, certified, or tenured people

Credentials held by the company.

Management beyond the owner

Leaders who hold the client relationships.

No client above a fifth of revenue

One dominant account lowers the price.

Margins that held through the downturn

Proof that pricing and staffing can adjust.

Industry reading

ACEC Research Institute

August 27, 2026

Engineering firms hold a year of work and expect a slower economy

The third-quarter survey of engineering firms recorded a median backlog of 12 months, with the strongest sentiment in data centers and in water and wastewater. The same firms put the odds of a recession within six months at 41%, and 88% reported open positions.

Backlog and a bench of licensed engineers are the first two facts a buyer will test.

Staffing Industry Analysts

September 29, 2026

Staffing revenue grows 5% at the median, the best reading since 2022

The August Pulse Survey of 128 staffing firms recorded median revenue growth of 5% from a year earlier. Engineering staffing led at 10% and industrial staffing followed at 8%, after three years in which industry revenue declined.

The recovery is strongest in the skilled categories where this year's acquisitions are concentrated.

Read the article

Insurance Journal

August 20, 2026

Commercial premiums fall 2% as property pricing softens

The Council of Insurance Agents & Brokers recorded an average premium decline of 2% in the second quarter. Large accounts fell 3.7% and small accounts fell 0.5%, and commercial property fell 6.3% while umbrella and commercial auto continued to rise.

Account size and line of business now explain most of the difference in agency growth.

Economy and deal market

Reuters, via Investing.com

October 1, 2026

Global deal value falls 41% from the second quarter

Announced global M&A fell to $993 billion in the third quarter, down 41% from the second quarter and the first quarter under $1 trillion since mid-2025. Year-to-date value of $3.9 trillion is the highest for the period since 2001 (LSEG data).

The slowdown is concentrated in the largest transactions. Add-on purchases by existing platforms made up about three-quarters of second-quarter buyouts by count (PitchBook).

September 16, 2026

The Federal Open Market Committee raises its target range

The committee raised the target range for the federal funds rate by 25 basis points to 3.75% to 4.00% and stated that inflation remains elevated. Senior debt on middle-market acquisitions was already priced at 7.8% in the second quarter.

Lenders are advancing less debt against each dollar of earnings, with average total debt at 2.9x EBITDA on platform deals (GF Data via ACG).

Smaller transactions reach their highest multiple since early 2022

The quarterly survey of business brokers and M&A advisors put the average multiple on sales of $5 million to $50 million at 5.8x EBITDA, up from 5.5x a year earlier and the highest since the first quarter of 2022. Sellers received 83% to 92% of the price in cash at closing.

Competition for well-prepared businesses is intact, with three or more offers on 87% of sales above $5 million (survey release).
