Market Conditions

Six developments across the five sectors, fourth quarter 2026

  • Fire and life safety consolidation. Pye-Barker Fire & Safety completed 22 acquisitions in the first half of 2026 (Pye-Barker) after 57 in 2025 (Pye-Barker, March 17). APi Group grew 10.1% organically in the second quarter and reported a backlog above $5 billion (APi Group).
  • Acquirers compete most actively for contracted inspection and service work.
  • 10-year Treasury yield. The Federal Reserve raised its target range to 3.75% to 4.00% on September 16 (Federal Reserve), and the 10-year Treasury yield averaged 4.99% in September (FRED). Average total debt on platform acquisitions fell to 2.9x EBITDA from 3.4x (GF Data via ACG).
  • Costlier debt puts a premium on documented earnings and contracted customers.
  • Engineering backlog and staffing. The median engineering firm holds 12 months of backlog, and 88% of firms report at least one open position (ACEC). Bernhard Capital agreed to buy Bowman for about $1.0 billion of enterprise value, a 58% premium (Bowman).
  • Buyers of engineering firms are paying for licensed staff and funded backlog.
  • Commercial insurance pricing. Commercial premiums fell 2% on average in the second quarter, with property down 6.3% (Insurance Journal). Aon agreed on August 31 to buy USI from KKR for $17 billion including debt (Insurance Journal, September 21).

Our View

Expectations for the fourth quarter and the first half of 2027

  • The lower middle market has decoupled from the headline deal market. Global M&A fell 41% from the second quarter on the rate move, but the decline sits in the largest transactions.
  • 3.75% to 4.00%. Federal funds target after the September 16 increase.
  • 2.9x. Average total debt on platform deals, down from 3.4x.
  • 87%. Sales above $5 million that drew three or more offers.
  • Structure before price. With platform leverage at 2.9x and senior debt priced at 7.8%, buyers close the gap with rollover equity, earnouts, and seller notes rather than lower headline numbers.
  • Timing. The Fed raised rates into a labor market adding 66,000 jobs a month, with unemployment at 4.2% and engineering executives putting recession odds at 41%.
  • Commercial & facility. Fire and life safety stays the most contested corner of business services, and the premium narrows to inspection-led, contracted revenue with company-held licenses, which is what Pye-Barker, APi, and AI Fire are paying for.

Sector by Sector

Five Sectors

Buyers, drivers, and valuation tests by sector

  • Commercial & facility services. Contracted, inspection-driven revenue is drawing the most active consolidators.
  • Market size and growth. Recurring monthly revenue at the 100 largest security dealers rose 7% to $740 million in 2025 (SDM), and SGS grew 5.6% organically in the first half (SGS).
  • Active buyers. Pye-Barker, APi Group, AI Fire, and Cintas in fire and safety; Allied Universal in guarding; DEX Imaging and UBEO in office technology; SGS and Phenna Group in testing.
  • What buyers reward. Inspection, monitoring, and service contracts that renew each year, and licenses held by the company.
  • Marketing, sales & tech-enabled services. Advertising demand is strong, and specialists with data or a vertical focus are in demand.
  • Market size and growth. US internet advertising revenue reached $294.6 billion in 2025, up 13.9% (IAB and PwC), and North American promotional products sales reached a record $27.7 billion (ASI).
  • Active buyers. Sponsor-backed agency platforms such as OneMagnify, Power Digital, and Wpromote; Publicis and Havas; Accenture and Cognizant; HALO and Proforma in promotional products.

Fire and Life Safety

Why inspection and service revenue draws acquirers

  • 22. Pye-Barker acquisitions in the first half of 2026, after 57 in 2025 (Pye-Barker; Pye-Barker, March 17)
  • 10.1%. APi Group organic revenue growth in the second quarter, with backlog above $5 billion (APi Group)
  • 60%. Share of revenue APi aims to earn from inspection, service, and monitoring (APi Group, December 10, 2025)
  • $740M. Recurring monthly revenue of the 100 largest security dealers in 2025, up 7% (SDM)
  • Pye-Barker alone bought 79 fire and security companies in the eighteen months to June 2026, and APi Group, Sciens Building Solutions, and AI Fire are buying alongside it.
  • The work is contracted or mandated. Inspection, testing, and monitoring repeat on a schedule, so the revenue continues when new construction slows.
  • Acquired branches add density. A buyer that already runs routes in a region can serve an acquired book of accounts with the technicians it has.

Indicators to Watch

Growth, hiring, rates, and services employment through September 2026

  • Real GDP growth. The economy grew at a 2.2% annual rate in the second quarter of 2026 after 2.5% in the first.
  • Unemployment rate. Down 0.5 points since September 2021, with its low in April 2023.
  • Unemployment stood at 4.2% in September 2026, compared with 4.4% a year earlier.
  • Monthly change in payroll employment. Employers added 29,000 jobs in September, and the average over the past six months was 66,000.
  • 10-year Treasury yield and federal funds rate. The 10-year yield averaged 4.99% in September, up from 4.12% a year earlier, while the federal funds rate averaged 3.75%.
  • Temporary help services employment. Down 14% since September 2021, peaking in March 2022 and with its low in December 2025.
  • Temporary employment of 2.49 million is 21% below its March 2022 peak and slightly above its level a year earlier.

What We're Reading

Six sources from the third quarter

  • ACEC Research Institute: engineering firms hold a year of work and expect a slower economy. The third-quarter survey of engineering firms recorded a median backlog of 12 months, with the strongest sentiment in data centers and in water and wastewater. The same firms put the odds of a recession within six months at 41%, and 88% reported open positions.
  • Backlog and a bench of licensed engineers are the first two facts a buyer will test.
  • Staffing Industry Analysts: staffing revenue grows 5% at the median, the best reading since 2022. The August Pulse Survey of 128 staffing firms recorded median revenue growth of 5% from a year earlier. Engineering staffing led at 10% and industrial staffing followed at 8%, after three years in which industry revenue declined.
  • The recovery is strongest in the skilled categories where this year's acquisitions are concentrated.
  • Insurance Journal: commercial premiums fall 2% as property pricing softens. The Council of Insurance Agents & Brokers recorded an average premium decline of 2% in the second quarter. Large accounts fell 3.7% and small accounts fell 0.5%, and commercial property fell 6.3% while umbrella and commercial auto continued to rise.
  • Account size and line of business now explain most of the difference in agency growth.
  • Reuters, via Investing.com: global deal value falls 41% from the second quarter. Announced global M&A fell to $993 billion in the third quarter, down 41% from the second quarter and the first quarter under $1 trillion since mid-2025. Year-to-date value of $3.9 trillion is the highest for the period since 2001 (LSEG data).
