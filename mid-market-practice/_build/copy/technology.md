### What factors influence the valuation of a technology company?

Technology valuations reflect financial performance, revenue quality, customer retention, competitive differentiation, growth prospects, and the sustainability of cash flows.

For software businesses, acquirers frequently evaluate annual recurring revenue, net revenue retention, gross margin, customer acquisition efficiency, and long-term growth potential. For IT services and technology infrastructure businesses, greater emphasis may be placed on normalized EBITDA, contract quality, workforce utilization, operating leverage, capital requirements, and cash flow conversion.

Transaction valuations also reflect market conditions, strategic acquisition rationale, and company-specific risks.

### How are SaaS businesses valued differently from IT services companies?

SaaS businesses with subscription-based revenue, strong customer retention, and scalable delivery models may be evaluated using enterprise value-to-revenue or ARR multiples, alongside profitability and cash flow considerations.

IT services businesses are more commonly assessed using EBITDA-based valuation methodologies, with particular emphasis on recurring service contracts, gross margin quality, customer concentration, utilization, and earnings sustainability.

The appropriate methodology ultimately depends on business model, growth profile, profitability, capital intensity, and comparable transaction evidence.

### Who are the primary acquirers of middle-market technology companies?

The technology buyer universe includes strategic software and technology companies, industry consolidators, private equity-backed operating platforms, and qualified financial acquirers pursuing controlling ownership positions.

Acquisition strategies vary by subsector. Software consolidators may prioritize recurring revenue and customer retention, while strategic technology buyers may emphasize complementary products, intellectual property, customer access, and integration opportunities.

Financial sponsors typically evaluate sustainable earnings, management depth, growth efficiency, cash generation, and the potential for operational improvement or acquisition-led expansion.

### How should a technology business owner prepare for a sale?

Owners should begin by assessing normalized financial performance, revenue composition, customer retention, growth efficiency, intellectual property ownership, management infrastructure, and potential diligence risks.

For software businesses, preparation should include validation of recurring revenue metrics, customer cohort analysis, product architecture, cybersecurity practices, and development expense treatment.

For technology services businesses, important considerations include contract quality, customer concentration, employee utilization, talent retention, and sustainable operating margins.

Early preparation helps establish a credible investment thesis and supports an efficient transaction process.

### Can technology founders retain equity following a sale?

Certain qualifying controlling ownership transactions may allow existing shareholders to retain a minority ownership interest following closing.

Retained ownership can provide an opportunity to participate in future business performance while realizing a portion of the value created. The appropriate structure depends on buyer objectives, shareholder preferences, transaction economics, and applicable regulatory requirements.

## Key Themes Influencing Technology M&A

### 01 | Vertical Software Consolidation

Fragmented vertical software markets present opportunities for strategic acquirers and software-focused investment platforms to expand their portfolios through acquisitions.

Businesses with deeply embedded applications, high customer retention, recurring revenue, and differentiated industry functionality can be particularly attractive acquisition candidates. Buyers evaluate the durability of customer relationships, product relevance, market penetration, and opportunities for cross-selling or product expansion.

### 02 | Recurring Revenue Quality & Growth Efficiency

Recurring revenue remains an important valuation consideration across software and technology-enabled services businesses. Institutional investors increasingly differentiate between headline growth and the quality, profitability, and sustainability of that growth.

Buyers evaluate annual recurring revenue, net revenue retention, gross revenue retention, customer acquisition economics, gross margins, cash flow generation, and capital requirements. The balance between growth, profitability, and customer retention can materially influence valuation and transaction structure.

### 03 | Artificial Intelligence & Technology Differentiation

Artificial intelligence is influencing technology acquisition strategies as buyers assess both the opportunities and risks associated with emerging capabilities.

Businesses with proprietary data, defensible intellectual property, deeply integrated workflows, and clear opportunities to deploy AI-enabled functionality may offer differentiated strategic value. Conversely, investors increasingly scrutinize product substitutability, competitive disruption, implementation costs, and the sustainability of existing software economics.

### 04 | Cybersecurity, Cloud Infrastructure & Managed Services

Ongoing enterprise investment in cybersecurity, cloud infrastructure, IT modernization, and outsourced technology management supports acquisition interest across managed services and infrastructure software.

Strategic and financial acquirers assess these businesses based on contract quality, recurring revenue, customer retention, technical expertise, service delivery infrastructure, and opportunities to expand service offerings across existing customer relationships.

### 05 | Technology Services Consolidation & Operational Scalability

Fragmented IT consulting, software development, and technology services markets create opportunities for strategic consolidation and platform development.

Acquirers evaluate technical specialization, management depth, workforce utilization, client concentration, geographic delivery capabilities, and the scalability of operating infrastructure. Businesses with differentiated capabilities, strong client relationships, and sustainable margins can represent attractive acquisition opportunities.
