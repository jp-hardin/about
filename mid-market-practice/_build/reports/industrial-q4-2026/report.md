# Industrial M&A Report | Q4 2026

Manufacturing & Industrial Technology · Industrial Distribution & Services · Building Products & Construction Services · Energy, Power & Environmental Services · Transportation & Logistics

October 2026 · Jared Hardin, Managing Director, Benchmark International

## Overview

Middle-market industrial companies enter the fourth quarter with full order books and a deal market that has become more selective about what it pays for. American manufacturing has expanded for nine consecutive months, with the ISM Manufacturing PMI at 54.5 in September and its order backlog index up 4.6 points to 56.4 ([ISM](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/manufacturing-pmi-at-54-5-september-2026-ism-manufacturing-pmi-report-302894520.html)). Global deal value for the first nine months of the year reached $3.9 trillion, the highest for the period since 2001, and third-quarter value then fell 41% from the second quarter to $993 billion as long-term borrowing costs rose ([LSEG data reported by Reuters](https://www.globalbankingandfinance.com/global-m-deal-rush-fades-third-quarter-rising-borrowing/); [BOL News](https://bolnews.com/business/global-ma-activity-falls-41-to-below-1-trillion-in-third-quarter)). Strategic acquirers and private equity platforms are still buying machining, distribution, contracting, environmental and logistics capacity that already exists, because skilled labor is scarce and new equipment takes a long time to arrive, and they are paying the most for companies tied to power, data centers, aerospace and service work that codes and regulations require.

> "In 2026 and beyond, the industrials space is expected to see strong investor interest fueled by many long-term themes, including onshoring and nearshoring, electrification, infrastructure investment, digital transformation and technology adoption, sustainability, and others." ([Harris Williams, Outlook 2026: Industrials](https://www.harriswilliams.com/our-insights/ind-2026-industry-outlook-m&a-trends-industrials))

- **Manufacturing & Industrial Technology.** Orders for manufacturing technology reached $4.03 billion in the first seven months of 2026, 37.1% ahead of the same period of 2025 ([AMT data reported by MDM](https://www.mdm.com/news/research/economic-trends/u-s-metalworking-machinery-orders-slide-in-july/)), and North American electronics manufacturing services bookings in August were 53.5% above a year earlier ([Global Electronics Association](https://www.electronics.org/news-release/north-american-ems-orders-outpace-shipments-august-bookings-rise-535-year-over-year)).
- **Industrial Distribution & Services.** Sales by wholesalers of machinery, equipment and supplies were 12.1% higher in July than a year earlier ([Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/wholesale/pdf/mwts/currentwhl.pdf)). Refinery turnaround work is the soft spot, with Team, Inc. reporting turnaround activity down 51% in the first half ([Team, Inc.](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/318833/000031883326000053/a8-kex991q2x2026earnings.htm)).
- **Building Products & Construction Services.** Total construction spending in August was 1.7% below a year earlier ([Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/construction/c30/current/index.html)), while contractors doing data center work reported 9.9 months of backlog against 8.3 months for those that are not ([ABC](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/09/15/3362305/0/en/abc-survey-1-in-6-members-perform-data-center-work-backlog-rebounds-in-august.html)).
- **Energy, Power & Environmental Services.** The Energy Information Administration expects US electricity sales to grow about 2% in 2026 and again in 2027 ([EIA](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/report/elec_coal_renew.php)), and Clean Harbors agreed to two emergency response and field service acquisitions worth a combined $775 million within three weeks of each other ([Clean Harbors](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000822818/000082281826000031/clh-q22026pressrelease.htm); [Waste Advantage](https://wasteadvantagemag.com/clean-harbors-to-acquire-enviroserve/)).
- **Transportation & Logistics.** The Cass Freight Index for shipments rose 2.1% from a year earlier in August, its first annual increase since January 2023, and the truckload linehaul index was up 11.3% ([Cass Information Systems](https://www.cassinfo.com/freight-audit-payment/cass-transportation-indexes/august-2026)).
- **Pricing and financing.** The average multiple in GF Data's sponsor-reported sample was 7.0 times trailing adjusted EBITDA in the second quarter against 7.3 times in the first, and average total debt on platform acquisitions fell to 2.9 times EBITDA from 3.4 times ([GF Data via ACG](https://www.acg.org/news-trends/news/gf-data-reports-show-steady-middle-market-deal-flow-amid-more-selective)). The Federal Reserve then raised its target range to 3.75% to 4.00% on September 16 ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm)).
- **Benchmark International.** In the 24 months to September 30, 2026, Benchmark International closed 85 transactions for companies in the five industrial segments covered in this report, and it has 212 industrial engagements in progress.

## Where We Focus

Benchmark International's industrial coverage is organized into five segments. Each section below describes current demand in the segment's sub-sectors, the transactions announced in 2026, what was disclosed about pricing, and Benchmark's own activity.

### Manufacturing & Industrial Technology

*Engineered products and precision manufacturing where proprietary processes, certifications and approved-vendor status create durable advantages.*

Certified machine shops, electronics assemblers and flow control manufacturers are being assembled into platforms that serve aerospace, defense, medical, power and data center customers, and the order data explain why acquirers are in a hurry to add capacity.

- **Precision machining and fabrication.** US orders for manufacturing technology were $606 million in July, 55.2% above July 2025, and July was the fifth consecutive month above $500 million, which has happened only once before since the series began in 1998. Contract machine shops are increasing their investment faster than the market as a whole ([AMT data reported by MDM](https://www.mdm.com/news/research/economic-trends/u-s-metalworking-machinery-orders-slide-in-july/)). The Gardner Business Index for metalworking was 54.8 in August, its eighth consecutive month of expansion ([Modern Machine Shop](https://www.mmsonline.com/articles/metalworking-index-softens-in-august-but-production-strengthens)). Sentiment among metalformers cooled in September, when 29% expected incoming orders to rise over the following three months, down from 49% in August, while daily shipping levels held steady ([PMA](https://www.pma.org/press-release/september-2026-business-conditions-report-metalformers-optimism-declines-shipping-remains-steady/)).
- **Aerospace and defense components.** Boeing delivered 171 commercial airplanes in the second quarter, 14% more than a year earlier, and reported a commercial backlog of more than 6,200 airplanes ([Boeing](https://boeing.mediaroom.com/2026-07-28-Boeing-Reports-Second-Quarter-Results)). Airbus delivered 475 aircraft in the first eight months of the year ([Airbus](https://www.airbus.com/en/products-services/commercial-aircraft/orders-and-deliveries)). Nearly every 2026 machining announcement leads with the seller's AS9100, Nadcap or ITAR registrations.
- **Electronics and microelectronics.** North American electronics manufacturing services bookings in August were 53.5% above a year earlier and the three-month book-to-bill ratio was 1.39 ([Global Electronics Association](https://www.electronics.org/news-release/north-american-ems-orders-outpace-shipments-august-bookings-rise-535-year-over-year)). The printed circuit board book-to-bill was 1.46 in July ([Global Electronics Association](https://www.electronics.org/news-release/north-american-pcb-demand-strengthens-july-bookings-surge-62)). Global semiconductor sales passed $1 trillion for the year through August ([SIA](https://www.semiconductors.org/year-to-date-global-semiconductor-sales-top-1-trillion-through-august/)), and semiconductor equipment billings set a second consecutive quarterly record at $40.53 billion ([SEMI](https://www.semi.org/en/semi-press-release/global-semiconductor-equipment-billings-increased-23-percent-year-over-year-in-q2-2026-semi-reports)). Sanmina told investors it sees "strong demand for fiscal 2027, with growth ramping throughout the year and into fiscal 2028" ([Sanmina](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000897723/000089772326000036/sanmina_exx991xjune272026.htm)).
- **Plastics and molding.** The US plastics industry shipped $533.6 billion of product in 2025 and employed 1,049,200 people directly ([Plastics Industry Association](https://www.plasticsindustry.org/newsroom/plastics-industry-size-impact-report-2026-jobs/)). Prices for nearly all commodity resins were flat to lower heading into October ([Plastics Technology](https://www.ptonline.com/articles/october-2026-a-mostly-flat-to-down-price-trajectory-for-volume-resins)). Medical molding is where buyers are most active, with Command Medical Products and Banner Capital both announcing acquisitions in September and October.
- **Coatings and metal finishing.** Finishing shops with aerospace approvals are being combined into multi-site platforms. Talica, backed by JLL Partners, acquired Hard Anodize, a Nadcap and AS9100 anodizing shop, in August ([PCI Magazine](https://www.pcimag.com/articles/115073-talica-acquires-hard-anodize-expands-metal-finishing-platform)), and Madison Lake Capital combined finishing operations in Wisconsin and Arizona under the UltiCoat name in September ([Products Finishing](https://www.pfonline.com/news/ulticoat-unites-wisconsin-arizona-industrial-finishing-operations-under-one-brand)).
- **Pumps, vacuum and flow control.** Strategic buyers paid full prices for pump and actuator manufacturers in the third quarter. Franklin Electric bought Cat Pumps, Crane agreed to buy Trillium's US water pump business, and ABB offered about $5.5 billion for Rotork.
- **Automation and controls.** North American companies ordered 8,940 robots worth $622 million in the second quarter, 21.3% more by value than a year earlier, and customers outside the automotive industry placed 56% of the orders ([A3 data reported by The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/q2-2026-robotics-demand-increased-across-industries-reports-a3/)). Rockwell Automation's sales rose 8% to $2.31 billion in its June quarter, and it named semiconductor, data center and warehouse automation as its strongest markets ([Rockwell Automation](https://www.rockwellautomation.com/en-us/company/news/press-releases/Rockwell-Automation-Reports-Third-Quarter-2026-Results.html)).

Skilled labor limits how quickly a shop can respond to this demand. The Bureau of Labor Statistics projects about 30,400 openings a year for machinists and tool and die makers, all of them to replace workers who retire or move on ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/production/machinists-and-tool-and-die-makers.htm)). An acquirer buying a staffed, certified shop is buying a workforce that cannot be hired in the open market at any reasonable speed.

> "Subsectors including semiconductors, power and cooling infrastructure, as well as industrial companies tied to data center investments, benefited either directly or indirectly from AI infrastructure spending as the surging power requirements triggered major infrastructure investment needs and utility-scale power agreements." ([Lincoln International, Q2 2026 Lincoln Private Market Index](https://www.lincolninternational.com/publications/research-indices/q2-2026-lincoln-private-market-index/))

Price was disclosed only in the larger transactions, and those disclosures show how much strategic buyers will pay for engineered products with high margins. Franklin Electric's upfront payment for Cat Pumps equals about 7.8 times the $45 million of adjusted EBITDA Cat Pumps earned in 2025 on roughly $115 million of revenue. Crane described its price for the Trillium pump business as about 14.6 times estimated 2026 adjusted EBITDA, ABB's offer values Rotork at 19.5 times EBITDA, and nVent put its upfront payment for Maverick Power at 11.5 times anticipated 2026 adjusted EBITDA. Howmet Aerospace, which agreed in December 2025 to buy Consolidated Aerospace Manufacturing from Stanley Black & Decker for about $1.8 billion, stated its price as about 13 times expected 2026 adjusted EBITDA after synergies and tax benefits ([Howmet Aerospace](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/howmet-aerospace-to-acquire-consolidated-aerospace-manufacturing-from-stanley-black--decker-for-approximately-1-8-billion-302648005.html)). None of the founder-owned or family-owned sellers in the table disclosed terms, which is typical at that size.

The instructive transaction for an owner of an integration or engineering firm is GE Vernova's purchase of Robotech. Robotech was already working inside GE Vernova's plants, and its customer bought the firm to secure engineering capacity for its own factory expansion. Engineering talent is the scarce asset in this segment, and a company's value rests on how much of that talent stays after the founder sells.

### Industrial Distribution & Services

*Value-added distributors and field-service providers with recurring maintenance revenue and dense local relationships.*

Distributors are reporting their strongest sales growth in several years, and nearly every product line now has a specialist acquirer that is buying founder-led and second-generation companies several times a year. On the service side, work that codes require or that power and data center customers are funding is growing, while refinery turnaround work has been pushed into 2027.

- **MRO and parts distribution.** Fastenal's daily sales rose 14.7% in the second quarter and 16.6% in August, with sales to heavy manufacturing customers up 21.8% ([Fastenal](https://s23.q4cdn.com/591718779/files/doc_financials/2026/Q2/EX_99-1-06-30-2026-Earnings-Release-Final-no-Footers.pdf); [August sales reported by MDM](https://www.mdm.com/news/top-distributor-sectors/contractor/august-was-fastenals-best-sales-growth-in-4-years/)). Grainger's second-quarter sales were $5.02 billion, up 10.3% ([MDM](https://www.mdm.com/news/operations/earnings/grainger-tops-5b-in-2q-sales-raises-forecast/)), Applied Industrial Technologies grew 10.4% in its June quarter and DXP Enterprises grew 15.6% ([Applied](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000109563/000010956326000031/a8k8132026releaseexhibit991.htm); [DXP](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001020710/000162828026054167/earningsrelease6302026.htm)). Across the wider channel, sales by wholesalers of machinery, equipment and supplies were $59.9 billion in July, 12.1% above a year earlier, and their inventories-to-sales ratio fell to 2.66 from 2.91 ([Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/wholesale/pdf/mwts/currentwhl.pdf)). Price accounts for much of the growth at some distributors: MSC Industrial's average daily sales rose 7.8% in its March to May quarter, of which 7.2 points came from price ([MDM](https://www.mdm.com/news/operations/earnings/msc-reports-healthy-spring-sales-growth-acceleration-as-volume-flips-to-positive/)).
- **Gases and specialty supplies.** The industrial gas producers reported mid-single-digit growth in the Americas, with Linde's regional sales up 7% and Air Products' up 5% ([Linde](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001707925/000165495426007052/lin_ex991.htm); [Air Products](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000002969/000000296926000034/exhibit99130jun26.htm)), and Air Liquide reported a 6.7% price effect in its Americas merchant business in the second quarter ([Air Liquide](https://www.airliquide.com/sites/airliquide.com/files/2026-07/h1-2026-results-acceleration-second-quarter-air-liquide-combines-growth-continuous-performance_6a683bddd0a4b.pdf)). Independent gas and welding supply distributors are being bought by sponsor-backed consolidators. Meritus Gas Partners announced at least five acquisitions in 2026, four of them since July, and Falcon Gases, a new platform backed by Odyssey Investment Partners, was formed around Encore Gas & Supply in September.
- **Hydraulics and fluid power.** US fluid power shipments were 4.7% higher in June than a year earlier, with pneumatic shipments up 12.8% ([NFPA data reported by Power & Motion](https://www.powermotiontech.com/hydraulics/media-gallery/55396879/national-fluid-power-association-nfpa-fluid-power-shipments-rose-in-june-2026)). Parker Hannifin reported organic growth of 4.9% and a 16% increase in orders in its North American industrial businesses for the June quarter, with a record backlog of $12.8 billion ([Parker Hannifin](https://www.financialcontent.com/article/gnwcq-2026-8-6-parker-reports-record-fiscal-2026-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-results)).
- **Fire and life safety.** APi Group reported "solid growth in inspection, service, and monitoring revenues" and raised its full-year outlook ([APi Group](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001796209/000162828026050806/apg-20260630xexx991.htm)). Pye-Barker Fire & Safety completed 22 acquisitions in the first half of 2026 alone ([Pye-Barker](https://pyebarkerfs.com/?p=22122)), and Sciens Building Solutions, backed by Carlyle, bought its ninth Florida company in March ([Sciens](https://www.sciensusa.com/news/acquires-ninth-florida-company/)). Inspection and testing work that fire codes require gives these businesses recurring revenue, and that is what the consolidators are paying for.
- **Industrial maintenance and turnarounds.** Team, Inc., which depends on refinery and petrochemical turnarounds, reported second-quarter revenue down 7.8% and turnaround activity down 51% for the first half ([Team, Inc.](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/318833/000031883326000053/a8-kex991q2x2026earnings.htm)). Industrial Info Resources reported in September that refiners had moved maintenance on ten crude units at seven refineries from the fall of 2026 to the fall of 2027 while margins are high ([IIR Energy](https://www.industrialinfo.com/iirenergy/industry-news/article/some-us-refiners-deferring-maintenance-from-2026-to-2027--362613)). Contractors with power, utility and data center customers are in a different position. ### Energy, Power & Environmental Services

*Businesses serving power generation, water, energy transition and regulated environmental markets with non-discretionary demand.*

Electricity demand is growing at about 2% a year, the equipment needed to meet it is sold out years ahead, and the large environmental service companies are using acquisitions to add emergency response and field service capacity. Each of those conditions is drawing acquirers toward smaller companies that already hold the technicians, permits and customer relationships.

### Transportation & Logistics

*Carriers, 3PLs and specialized logistics providers with diversified shippers and capabilities buyers can't easily replicate.*

The freight market turned in 2026 after more than three years of contraction, and the recovery has come from carriers leaving the market more than from shippers moving more goods. Rates and carrier margins have improved as a result, fuel has become the largest cost risk, and acquirers are favoring dedicated contract fleets, customs and cross-border capability, and specialized services over general truckload capacity.

- **Dedicated and intermodal trucking.** The Cass Freight Index for shipments rose 2.1% from a year earlier in August, the first annual increase since January 2023, while freight expenditures rose 18.7% and the truckload linehaul index rose 11.3% ([Cass Information Systems](https://www.cassinfo.com/freight-audit-payment/cass-transportation-indexes/august-2026)). The American Trucking Associations' tonnage index was 1.6% lower than a year earlier in the same month, and its chief economist said the market "has certainly flipped this year, but recent tonnage levels confirm this is due to reduced capacity, not robust demand" ([ATA](https://www.trucking.org/news-insights/ata-truck-tonnage-index-fell-05-august)). Spot van rates averaged $2.19 a mile before fuel in August, more than 30% above August 2025, and contract rates averaged $2.41 ([DAT](https://www.dat.com/company/news-events/news-releases/dat-spot-van-rate-falls-20-cents-in-steepest-august-pullback-on-record)). J.B. Hunt's second-quarter revenue rose 19%, with intermodal revenue up 22% and dedicated revenue up 9% ([J.B. Hunt](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000728535/000143774926023629/ex_987956.htm)), and Knight-Swift's revenue rose 12.6% as revenue per loaded mile in its truckload business rose 5.5% ([Knight-Swift](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001492691/000149269126000055/exhibit99106302026.htm)). US rail intermodal volume is 4.1% ahead of 2025 for the year to late September ([AAR data via AJOT](https://www.ajot.com/news/aar-reports-rail-traffic-for-the-week-ending-september-26-2026)).
- **Capacity and cost.** The Logistics Managers' Index put transportation prices at 90.0 and transportation capacity at 40.0 in August, the ninth consecutive month in which capacity contracted ([Logistics Managers' Index](https://www.the-lmi.com/august-2026-logistics-managers-index.html)). Federal enforcement is part of the reason. An industry coalition expects more than 194,000 holders of non-domiciled commercial licenses to become ineligible ([FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/?p=577305)), and English-proficiency violations again place drivers out of service ([CCJ](https://www.ccjdigital.com/regulations/safety-compliance/article/15833851/how-fmcsa-english-proficiency-impacts-trucking-capacity)). Diesel averaged $6.38 a gallon in the week of September 28 ([EIA](https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/gasdiesel/)), and North American orders for Class 8 trucks were 18,200 in August, 42% more than a year earlier, after a year of buying ahead of the 2027 emissions standard ([FTR data reported by FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/class-8-truck-demand-stays-hot-ahead-of-costly-2027-transition)).
- **3PL and customs brokerage.** Import volumes have held up through repeated tariff changes. The Port of Los Angeles handled 955,907 containers in August and a record 2.9 million across June, July and August ([FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/port-of-los-angeles-posts-record-2-9m-teus-eyes-strong-finish-to-2026)), and the National Retail Federation forecasts 25.7 million containers of imports for 2026, about 1% more than 2025 ([NRF Global Port Tracker via Textile World](https://www.textileworld.com/textile-world/2026/09/nrf-import-cargos-peak-season-not-over-yet/)). Trade with Mexico reached a monthly record of $94.8 billion in July, 27.5% above a year earlier, and $35.9 billion of it moved through Port Laredo ([Census data reported by FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/us-mexico-trade-smashes-monthly-record-at-nearly-95b)). Every change in tariff schedules adds classification and compliance work for customs brokers, and the Supreme Court's February ruling against the emergency tariffs did not set a refund mechanism, which has left brokers tracking liquidation dates and assembling entry records for their importers ([NCBFAA](https://www.ncbfaa.org/news-advocacy/ncbfaa-in-the-news/2026/02/27/attorneys--ieepa-refunds-process-unclear--but-importers-can-still-get-ready)). Logistics platforms have responded by buying brokers at the border, and Redwood Logistics and Imperative Logistics Group each acquired a founder-owned customs broker in Laredo this year.
- **Export crating and rigging.** Reshoring, data center construction and new plant construction create demand for machinery moving, crating and plant relocation, and these services sit close to the manufacturers described earlier in this report. Spending on data center construction was more than 73% higher in August than a year earlier ([ABC analysis reported by MDM](https://www.mdm.com/?p=436875)), and Manitowoc's crane orders rose 56.1% to $708.7 million in the second quarter ([Manitowoc](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000061986/000119312526337931/mtw-ex99_1.htm)). MEI Industrial Solutions, the former MEI Rigging & Crating owned by Olympus Partners, opened the year by acquiring CCI Industrial Services, a Kentucky rigging, machinery moving and millwright contractor with about 120 employees ([MEI Industrial Solutions](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/mei-industrial-solutions-acquires-cci-industrial-services-expanding-southeast-capabilities-and-establishing-a-presence-in-kentucky-302656685.html)). Omega Morgan bought an owner-operated machinery mover in Seattle, JK Technology Services bought a Virginia rigging company that works in data centers, Bragg Companies bought a heavy transport operator in Arizona, and Specialized Packaging Group bought an owner-operated crating business near Dallas.
- **Pallets and packaging logistics.** Volumes are soft and costs are rising, which favors acquirers with scale. UFP Industries' packaging segment grew sales 6.9% to $458.2 million in the second quarter while its adjusted EBITDA margin fell to 6.1% from 9.1%, and unit volume at its PalletOne business was 3% lower ([UFP Industries](https://www.ufpinvestor.com/node/31236)). Brambles reported a 2% decline in like-for-like pallet volumes at CHEP Americas for the year to June and noted tightening availability of quality whitewood pallets in the United States ([Brambles](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260820/pdf/072ytfq6ctnzgv.pdf)). The producer price index for wood pallets rose 4.7% between March and July ([BLS data via FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/WPU084101)). UFP bought two pallet manufacturers in the spring. It paid about $20 million including real estate for Berry Pallets of Waseca, Minnesota, which has about $23 million of revenue ([UFP Industries](https://ufpi.com/ufp-industries-expands-pallet-manufacturing-network-through-acquisition/)), and about $48 million for John Rock of Coatesville, Pennsylvania, which has about $82 million of sales ([UFP Industries](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ufp-industries-expands-palletones-northeast-footprint-through-acquisition-of-john-rock-inc-302761483.html)). In packaging distribution, SupplyOne announced four acquisitions of owner-operated or family-owned companies during the year and Veritiv announced at least three.
- **Marine and subsea.** Kirby's marine transportation revenue rose 9% to $537.0 million in the second quarter, with inland barge utilization in the low-90% range and coastal utilization in the high-90% range ([Kirby](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000056047/000119312526323594/kex-ex99_1.htm)). Offshore service activity is steady. Oceaneering's remotely operated vehicles were 66% utilized at an average of $11,894 a day in the second quarter ([Oceaneering](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000073756/000007375626000134/exhibit_991x8kx2qx2026.htm)), Tidewater's active fleet was 81.4% utilized at an average day rate of $22,938 ([Tidewater](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000098222/000143774926025451/ex_956944.htm)), and Helix's well intervention vessels were 91% utilized ([Helix Energy Solutions](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000866829/000086682926000021/hlx-20260806xex99d1.htm)). Ownership is consolidating among private and family-owned operators. Hines Furlong Line completed its purchase of Campbell Transportation's river division in February, which leaves it operating more than 800 barges and 64 towboats, Chouest bought a Louisiana decommissioning contractor, C-Dive bought Helix's shallow-water fleet, and Turn Services acquired St. John Fleeting on the Lower Mississippi in September.

Werner's purchase of FirstFleet is the clearest statement of what acquirers want. Werner pointed to an average tenure of 17 years among FirstFleet's ten largest customers ([Werner](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260127697920/en/Werner%C2%AE-Acquires-FirstFleet-Inc.-Expanding-Dedicated-Market-Leader)), and its chief executive said, "We've been very deliberate over the past few years about transitioning our portfolio increasingly towards the higher-margin dedicated trucking business" ([Transport Topics](https://www.ttnews.com/articles/werner-acquires-firstfleet)). The price for the operating business was roughly 0.4 times revenue, a reminder that asset-based carriers are valued on earnings and equipment and that long customer tenure is what distinguishes one fleet from another. Asset-light specialists were priced differently, and C.H. Robinson paid about $75 million for DeSpir Logistics, a secure transport provider with $62 million of revenue. UFP paid a little under one times revenue for Berry Pallets and about 0.6 times sales for John Rock, and Wynnchurch Capital's agreement to buy Arcosa's barge-building business for $450 million equals about 6.6 times the $68 million of adjusted segment EBITDA the business earned in 2025 ([WorkBoat](https://www.workboat.com/private-equity-to-buy-arcosas-barge-business-for-450-million)). None of the founder-owned or family-owned sellers disclosed an EBITDA multiple.

Most of the sellers in this list are founders and families, and several announcements describe an owner's retirement. The buyers wanted a capability or a location they did not have, such as a customs brokerage at the Laredo crossing, a food-grade bulk fleet in California or a drayage operation at the Port of Charleston. Gaurang Shastri, a managing director at Lincoln International, described the market to Transport Topics in July: "The simplest way to describe today's market is that buyers are acquiring capabilities rather than capacity" ([Lincoln International](https://www.lincolninternational.com/perspectives/in-the-press/transport-topics-transport-ma-builds-momentum-alongside-freight-market/)). Benchmark International's own recent transactions in this segment fit that description, because each acquirer was adding warehousing, cross-border or fulfillment capability it did not have. C.H. Robinson's agreement on October 5 to acquire RXO at an implied value of $5.8 billion, with about $300 million of expected annual cost savings, shows the same reasoning at the top of the brokerage market.

> "Strategic buyers and PE sponsors alike continue to prioritize these differentiated platforms because they command higher margins, boast sticky customer relationships in resilient end markets, and differentiate themselves with a level of service and capabilities that traditional scale-driven networks cannot match." ([Houlihan Lokey, Summer 2026 Transportation and Logistics Market Update](http://cdn.hl.com/pdf/2026/transportation-and-logistics-summer-2026-market-update.pdf))

**Benchmark International in this segment.** Benchmark closed 3 transactions for transportation and logistics companies in the 24 months to September 30, 2026, and has 14 engagements in progress. The closed transactions were a cross-border freight forwarding and customs brokerage group, a warehousing and transloading operator, and a fulfillment and distribution company. The engagements in progress include truckload and refrigerated carriers, intermodal drayage, freight brokerage and forwarding between the United States and Mexico, customs compliance and dedicated distribution services.

## What We're Seeing in Industrial

Four themes run through the transactions described above: reshoring and supply-chain resilience, platform building by private equity, infrastructure and electrification, and the value buyers place on service and aftermarket revenue. They are playing out in a deal market where financing became more expensive in September.

### 01 Reshoring and supply-chain resilience

Demand for domestic manufacturing capacity is broad, capacity is not yet tight, and input costs are rising quickly. The ISM Manufacturing PMI has been above 50 in every month of 2026.

{{chart:ism}}

The September ISM report showed new orders at 55.3, the backlog of orders at 56.4 and customers' inventories at 41.6, a level that points to further restocking. Twelve manufacturing industries reported growth and two reported contraction ([ISM](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/manufacturing-pmi-at-54-5-september-2026-ism-manufacturing-pmi-report-302894520.html)). S&P Global's separate manufacturing PMI for the same month was 55.9, the strongest reading since May 2022 ([S&P Global data via Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/manufacturing-pmi)).

The output data have moved more slowly than the surveys. Manufacturing capacity utilization was 75.7% in August, about 2.5 points below its long-run average ([Federal Reserve G.17](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g17/current/default.htm)), while core capital goods orders are up 10.6% for the year to date ([Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/manufacturing/m3/adv/pdf/durgd.pdf)). Manufacturing construction spending ran at an annual rate of $170.7 billion in August, 31.8% below its September 2024 peak and level with the prior two months ([Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/construction/c30/pdf/release.pdf); [historical series via FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/TLMFGCONS)). The factories started in 2023 and 2024 are now being equipped and staffed, which is where the demand for machine tools, automation and integration services is coming from.

Labor is the binding constraint on output, and manufacturing job openings were 522,000 in August, 25.5% above a year earlier ([BLS JOLTS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/jolts.t01.htm)), and a fabricated metals respondent told ISM, "Our biggest challenge continues to be a severe shortage of workers, limiting our production output to meet demand." Input costs are the main pressure on margins, and the ISM prices index rose to 77.9 in September, and rising raw material costs were the top concern in the National Association of Manufacturers' third-quarter survey for the second quarter in a row ([NAM](https://nam.org/2026-third-quarter-manufacturers-outlook-survey/)).

Trade policy has changed several times this year and remains unsettled. The Supreme Court ruled on February 20 that the International Emergency Economic Powers Act does not authorize tariffs ([case summary, Faegre Drinker](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/2/supreme-court-decides-learning-resources-inc-v-trump-and-trump-v-vos-selections-inc)). Since July 24 the baseline has been a Section 301 tariff of 10% or 12.5% on 60 trading partners ([USTR](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/fact-sheets/2026/july/fact-sheet-ustr-section-301-action-response-failure-60-economies-ban-imports-produced-forced-labor)), which the Court of International Trade heard a challenge to on September 30 ([Reuters](https://kfgo.com/2026/09/30/us-trade-court-to-weigh-challenge-to-trumps-forced-labor-tariffs/)). Section 232 duties of 50% on steel, aluminum and most copper articles remain in force ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/09/2026-06960/strengthening-actions-taken-to-adjust-imports-of-aluminum-steel-and-copper-into-the-united-states)), and the United States declined to renew the USMCA on July 1, and the agreement remains in force pending resolution of the issues raised in that review ([USTR](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/july/ambassador-greer-issues-statement-usmca-joint-review)). For a domestic manufacturer or distributor, the practical consequence is that buyers now ask for gross margin by month across each tariff change, because that record shows how quickly cost increases were passed through to customers.

### 02 Platform building by private equity

Private equity firms hold 13,509 US companies, and the companies they sold in the first half of 2026 had been owned for a median of 6.4 years. US private equity deal value was $461.0 billion in the first half, 10.6% below the first half of 2025, and an estimated 885 add-on acquisitions made up about three-quarters of all buyouts in the second quarter ([PitchBook Q2 2026 US PE Breakdown](https://pitchbook.brightspotcdn.com/40/42/bbeb88094080abaea3d7c87530b8/q2-2026-us-pe-breakdown.pdf)). Business-to-business companies, the category that holds most industrial and distribution businesses, produced $40.6 billion of exit value in the second quarter, roughly 45% of all realizations.

For a founder-owned industrial company, this means that the private equity buyer at the table is usually a platform the sponsor already owns. The 2026 transactions in this report show the pattern in every segment: Threadlock Precision and Precinmac in aerospace machining, Meritus Gas Partners in gas distribution, Pye-Barker with 22 fire and life safety acquisitions in six months, Motion & Control Enterprises with four fluid power acquisitions, Ridgeline in roofing and Blue Castle in waste collection. These buyers know the sub-sector, move quickly and can often offer the owner continued equity in the larger company.

> "Sponsors remain highly active and are competing hard for high-quality, stable assets. With substantial dry powder to deploy and a growing backlog of portfolio companies to exit, private equity is a powerful source of both demand and supply, adding to the depth and competitiveness of current processes." ([Baird, Global Industrial Group 2026 Mid-Year Update](https://www.rwbaird.com/corporations-and-institutions/investment-banking/insights/2026/07/global-industrial-groups-2026-mid-year-update/))

Houlihan Lokey, writing about packaging and plastics, expects "sponsors to remain focused on bolt-on strategies to build scale and prepare for a more active exit window in 2027" ([Houlihan Lokey, 1H 2026 Packaging, Plastics, and Paper Market Update](http://cdn.hl.com/pdf/2026/houlihan-lokey-packaging-plastics-paper-1h-2026-market-update.pdf)).

### 03 Infrastructure and electrification

Power and data center construction is the strongest source of demand across all five segments. It appears in the 116 gigawatts of gas turbine commitments at GE Vernova, the $14.06 billion backlog at Comfort Systems USA, the extra month and a half of backlog that ABC contractors report when they do data center work, the 29% growth in commercial and industrial generator sales at Generac, and the machine tool orders placed by electrical equipment manufacturers. Meta alone expects capital expenditures of $130 billion to $145 billion in 2026 ([Meta](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/meta-reports-second-quarter-2026-results-302838214.html)).

Public infrastructure funding is less certain than it was a year ago. The surface transportation authorization expired on September 30 and highway programs are operating under a continuing resolution through December 11, while the House committee's five-year successor bill awaits action in the Senate ([AASHTO Journal](https://aashtojournal.transportation.org/?p=41360); [Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/05/22/surface-transportation-bill-approved-by-house-committee/)). Contractors and suppliers whose work depends on federal highway money should expect buyers to ask how much of the backlog is already funded.

> "Investor interest remains strong for companies tied to a variety of megatrends, including the energy transition, electrification, and grid modernization; infrastructure upkeep and upgrades; and a heightened focus on sustainability, connectivity, and digitization." ([Harris Williams, Outlook 2026: Energy, Power & Infrastructure](https://www.harriswilliams.com/our-insights/epi-2026-industry-outlook-m&a-trends-energy-power-infrastructure))

### 04 Service and aftermarket revenue

Recurring service revenue is being valued more highly than project or product revenue in every segment covered here. Applied Industrial Technologies' Engineered Solutions segment grew 12.9% organically in its June quarter against 7.9% for its service centers ([Applied](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000109563/000010956326000031/a8k8132026releaseexhibit991.htm)), and Transcat's service revenue grew 27%. APi Group is working toward 60% of revenue from inspection and service ([APi Group](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251210396417/en/APi-Group-Announces-Acquisition-of-CertaSite-and-Provides-Full-Year-2025-Update)). When Rollins bought Romex Pest Control for $95.7 million, it assigned almost nine-tenths of the price to customer contracts and goodwill ([Rollins Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000084839/000008483926000040/rol-20260630.htm)), which shows what a buyer of recurring revenue is paying for.

The practical step for an owner is to report service, repair, inspection and aftermarket parts revenue separately from project and product sales, with the renewal history of the agreements behind it, because buyers pay for that revenue only when they can see it.

### The deal market and the cost of capital

Global M&A value reached a record $2.85 trillion in the first half of 2026, 50% above the first half of 2025, while the number of transactions fell 9%, and industrials deal activity rose 57% ([LSEG data reported by Investment Executive](https://www.investmentexecutive.com/news/markets/mega-deals-drive-global-ma-record/)). The third quarter was much slower, as value fell 41% from the second quarter to $993 billion, the first quarter below $1 trillion since the second quarter of 2025, and only ten transactions above $10 billion were announced ([LSEG data reported by Reuters](https://www.globalbankingandfinance.com/global-m-deal-rush-fades-third-quarter-rising-borrowing/)). Mergermarket, which uses a different dataset, counted $4.44 trillion for the first nine months, 27% above the prior year, on 1% fewer transactions, and described "a marked third-quarter slowdown" ([Mergermarket](https://ionanalytics.com/insights/mergermarket/ma-highlights-9m26/)). In industrial manufacturing, strategic acquirers accounted for 86% of deal value over the last twelve months in PwC's midyear review ([PwC](https://www.pwc.com/us/en/industries/industrial-products/library/industrial-manufacturing-deals-insights.html)).

Reuters attributed the slowdown to rising borrowing costs. The Federal Reserve held its target range at 3.50% to 3.75% through July and raised it to 3.75% to 4.00% on September 16 by a 12 to 0 vote ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm)). The 10-year Treasury yield was 5.31% on October 5 ([U.S. Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?type=daily_treasury_yield_curve&field_tdr_date_value_month=202610)). Lenders were already more cautious before the September move. GF Data reported that average total debt on platform acquisitions fell to 2.9 times trailing EBITDA in the second quarter from 3.4 times in the first, and that senior debt pricing rose to 7.8% from 7.2% ([GF Data via ACG](https://www.acg.org/news-trends/news/gf-data-reports-show-steady-middle-market-deal-flow-amid-more-selective)). Banks told the Federal Reserve in July that their standards for commercial and industrial loans were basically unchanged and that demand from middle-market firms had strengthened ([Senior Loan Officer Opinion Survey](https://www.federalreserve.gov/data/sloos/sloos-202607.htm)).

Private equity activity held up better in the third quarter than the global totals suggest. PitchBook's preliminary count for the quarter was 2,466 US private equity transactions worth $230.4 billion, 20% more by value than in the second quarter, with exits of $165.1 billion ([PitchBook data summarized by Foley & Lardner](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/10/an-early-look-at-q3-2026-pe-activity/)). Lenders expect terms to tighten from here. In PitchBook LCD's third-quarter survey of leveraged finance participants, 36% expected loan spreads to widen over the next six months, 38% expected leverage on buyouts to fall and 54% expected sponsors to be asked for more equity ([PitchBook LCD](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/q3-us-leveraged-finance-survey-geopolitical-turmoil-and-software-top-concerns)). Fitch Ratings put the default rate among private credit borrowers at 6.3% for the twelve months to August, and at 9.9% among industrial borrowers ([Fitch data reported by Global Finance](https://gfmag.com/private-credit/fed-rate-hike-squeezes-an-already-stressed-private-credit-sector/)), which helps explain why lenders are examining cyclical industrial credits closely.

Industrials deal value, which stood 57% ahead of the prior year at midyear, finished the first nine months 13% ahead ([LSEG data reported by Investment Executive](https://www.investmentexecutive.com/news/research-and-markets/global-ma-strong-in-2026/)). Large strategic acquirers have continued to commit capital since the quarter ended. On October 5, Schneider Electric agreed to buy the industrial software company PTC for $205 a share in cash, an equity value of about $22.6 billion ([Schneider Electric](https://www.se.com/ww/en/assets/pdf/Schneider-Electric-to-acquire-PTC)), and C.H. Robinson agreed to buy the freight broker RXO at an implied value of $5.8 billion, a 29% premium to RXO's last closing price ([C.H. Robinson and RXO](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001929561/000114036126038548/ef20083265_ex99-1.htm)).

With less debt in each purchase, buyers are funding a larger share with equity, and they are placing more weight on earnings quality and on structure such as rollover equity and earn-outs. Lincoln International's private market index for the second quarter recorded the same discipline, which Steve Kaplan of the University of Chicago Booth School of Business summarized in Lincoln's release: "Private company enterprise value growth was driven by operating performance, not multiple expansion" ([Lincoln International](https://www.lincolninternational.com/publications/research-indices/q2-2026-lincoln-private-market-index/)). Baird's industrial group, writing in early August before the rate increase, reported that "strategic buyers are leading our sell-side momentum" and that "in recent processes, nearly half of buyers have come from outside the U.S., with especially strong engagement from European acquirers" ([Baird](https://www.rwbaird.com/corporations-and-institutions/investment-banking/insights/2026/07/global-industrial-groups-2026-mid-year-update/)).

## Seeing Your Company Through a Buyer's Eyes

Three characteristics account for most of the difference between an average offer and a strong one for an industrial company, and the 2026 transactions in this report give evidence for each.

### 01 Engineering know-how

*Proprietary processes, certifications such as AS9100 and ISO, and engineered-to-order capabilities.*

Nearly every aerospace, defense and medical machining announcement this year named the seller's AS9100, Nadcap, ISO 13485 or ITAR registrations in its first paragraph, and the finishing and controls announcements did the same with Nadcap and UL 508A. Certifications take years to earn and customers audit them, so an acquirer cannot recreate them quickly. Engineered products also earn higher margins, and Cat Pumps reported an adjusted EBITDA margin above 35% when Franklin Electric acquired it. Where the know-how sits in people, as it does at an integrator or an engineering firm, buyers look closely at which employees hold it and whether they will stay.

### 02 Customer stickiness

*Approved-vendor status, long OEM relationships and recurring service agreements.*

Werner's announcement of the FirstFleet acquisition pointed to an average tenure of 17 years among FirstFleet's ten largest customers. Fire and life safety consolidators are paying for inspection agreements that fire codes require customers to renew, and calibration, water treatment service and waste collection share that characteristic. Customer concentration is the other side of the same question. A long relationship with one large customer is valuable, and a buyer will still reduce its offer if losing that customer would remove a large share of earnings, so owners should document contract terms, tenure and the breadth of contacts inside each major account.

### 03 Capacity and equipment

*Modern equipment, documented maintenance and room for a buyer to add volume.*

Manufacturing capacity utilization of 75.7% means most plants have room to take on more work, and contract machine shops are investing in new machine tools faster than the market as a whole. Lead times of 160 weeks for large transformers and sold-out production slots for gas turbines show why existing capacity in the power supply chain commands a premium. Permanent 100% bonus depreciation for qualified property acquired after January 19, 2025 also raises the value a buyer places on a step-up in the basis of a seller's equipment ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/working-families-tax-cuts-businesses)). Maintenance records, a current equipment list with ages and hours, and an honest statement of what the next owner will need to replace all shorten diligence.

A company that grows from $3 million to $12 million of EBITDA therefore gains on the earnings and again on the multiple applied to them.

## Advisory Built Around Your Goals

An exit can take many forms, and Benchmark International helps industrial owners choose the path that fits their wealth, family and company goals.

| Path | What it involves | Where the current market favors it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sell-side M&A | A full sale to the right strategic acquirer or financial sponsor, run as a confidential, competitive process from positioning through closing | Strategic acquirers bought 56% of the Benchmark industrial clients with a classified buyer in the past 24 months, and specialist platforms are active in every segment |
| Recapitalizations and partial sales | Taking cash out while keeping a stake in the upside, through majority or minority recapitalizations with private equity and family offices | Intermediaries in Pepperdine's 2026 survey reported rollover equity in 46% of the transactions they closed |
| Strategic partnerships and growth capital | Finding a partner that brings capital, capabilities or market access to accelerate the next phase of growth | Companies with data center, power and aerospace demand that exceeds their capacity or working capital |
| Exit planning and valuation | Understanding what the company is worth today, what drives value in its sector, and the steps that will raise it before going to market | Owners who expect to sell within two or three years and have time to address concentration, management depth and reporting |

### How transactions are being structured

Intermediaries responding to Pepperdine's 2026 survey reported using seller financing in 54% of the transactions they closed and rollover equity in 46%, and most said a sale took nine months or more to complete ([Pepperdine](https://digitalcommons.pepperdine.edu/gsbm_pcm_pcmr/19)). In the SRS Acquiom 2026 study of private-company acquisitions, 24% of deals included an earn-out with a median size of 34% of the payment at closing, and representations and warranties insurance was used in 46% ([Fasken](https://www.fasken.com/en/knowledge/2026/05/private-ma-deal-trends-to-watch-key-takeaways-from-srs-acquioms-2026-study)). That study draws on larger transactions than most founder-owned industrial companies will complete, so its figures are best read as a guide to what buyers are likely to ask for.

Contractors have two additional items to plan for. An owner's personal indemnity to the surety stays in force after a sale unless the surety releases it ([Construction Business Owner](https://www.constructionbusinessowner.com/strategy/what-know-about-bond-liability-selling-your-company)), and for companies that contribute to a multiemployer pension plan, an asset sale can trigger withdrawal liability unless the conditions of ERISA Section 4204 are met ([SMACNA](https://smacna.org/business-resources/resource/what-contractors-should-know-about-withdrawal-liability)).
