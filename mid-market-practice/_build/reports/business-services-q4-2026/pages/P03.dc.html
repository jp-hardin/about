BUSINESS SERVICES · Q4 2026

Deal market: a record first half, then a rate shock in the third quarter

Global announced M&A reached $2.8 trillion in the first half of 2026, up 48% on the prior year and the highest first-half total since LSEG began keeping records in 1980, although the number of deals fell 9% to roughly 24,000, a six-year low (Reuters, LSEG data, July 1, 2026). The third quarter reversed the trend in value: global volume fell 41% from the second quarter to $993 billion, the first quarter under $1 trillion since mid-2025, and Reuters attributed the drop to rising borrowing costs (Reuters, October 1, 2026). North America recorded 10,230 transactions worth $2.37 trillion through nine months, up 33%, with third-quarter value of $560.4 billion running 23% below the same quarter of 2025 (Mergermarket, October 1, 2026).

Borrowing costs rose sharply over the same months, as the Federal Reserve raised its target range by 25 basis points to 3.75% to 4.00% on September 16, 2026, stating that inflation remains elevated (Federal Reserve, FOMC statement). Consumer prices rose 3.4% over the twelve months to August, with energy up 16.3% (BLS, CPI), and the 10-year Treasury yield moved above 5% in mid-September (Vanguard, September 17, 2026). The underlying economy is still growing: real GDP rose at a 2.2% annual rate in the second quarter (BEA), unemployment stood at 4.2% in September with payrolls up 29,000 (BLS, Employment Situation, October 2, 2026), and banks reported commercial lending standards basically unchanged in the second quarter (Federal Reserve, Senior Loan Officer Opinion Survey, July 2026).

Private equity carries a large inventory of companies it needs to sell and a large pool of capital it needs to invest, and both conditions favor a well-prepared private seller. Bain counted 32,000 unsold portfolio companies worth $3.8 trillion at the end of 2025, almost 40% of them held for more than five years, alongside $1.3 trillion of buyout dry powder (Bain & Company, Global Private Equity Report 2026). US private equity deal value fell to $177.3 billion in the second quarter of 2026, down 37.5% from the first quarter, and add-on acquisitions made up about three-quarters of the buyout transactions completed in the quarter (PitchBook, Q2 2026 US PE Breakdown). PwC estimates that clearing the existing private equity inventory at the current pace would take nine years (PwC, US Deals 2026 Midyear Outlook). A purchase by a platform the sponsor already owns is smaller and easier to finance at current rates than a new platform investment, which is why sponsor-backed companies appear as the buyer in so many of the transactions in this report.
