Commercial insurance pricing turned negative in 2026, and the effect on an agency depends on the accounts and lines it writes. The Council of Insurance Agents & Brokers recorded an average premium decline of 2% in the second quarter, with large accounts down 3.7%, medium accounts down 1.9% and small accounts down 0.5%, and with commercial property down 6.3% while umbrella rose 5.3% and commercial auto rose 4.5% (Insurance Journal, August 20, 2026). Marsh's index showed property rates in the United States down 13% and casualty rates up 7% (Marsh, July 23, 2026). An agency weighted toward small commercial accounts and casualty lines is therefore seeing less pressure on commissions than one weighted toward large property accounts.

Organic growth at the listed brokers has slowed with pricing, and acquisitions account for most of the growth they report. Second-quarter organic growth was 7% in WTW's broking business, 6% at Gallagher, about 5% at Aon and 3% in Marsh's risk and insurance business (Insurance Business, July 31, 2026), and 6.7% at Ryan Specialty (Ryan Specialty, July 30, 2026). Brown & Brown's revenue rose 30.4% to $1.7 billion while its organic revenue fell 0.7% (Stock Titan, July 27, 2026), and Baldwin's revenue rose 30% with 2% organic growth after its $1.026 billion purchase of CAC Group in January (Insurance Business).

The largest transactions of 2026 have been purchases of the consolidators themselves. Aon agreed to buy USI Insurance Services from KKR for $17 billion in cash (Insurance Journal, September 21, 2026), and The Baldwin Group agreed to a majority sale to Sequence Holdings and DFO Management at $32.50 per share, in a transaction valued at $7.7 billion (Insurance Journal, October 5, 2026). S&P Global counted 652 insurance transactions in North America in 2025 with a combined value of $41.17 billion, compared with 674 transactions and $59.32 billion in 2024 (S&P Global, February 9, 2026).

Insurance brokerage and managing general agents. Premium written through managing general agents reached $108.7 billion in 2025, up 17.8% against 5% growth for the property and casualty industry, across nearly 800 firms, according to AM Best (Risk & Insurance, July 3, 2026). Surplus lines premium grew 2.8% to $47.6 billion in the first half of 2026 after growing 13.2% in the first half of 2025, with property premium down 13.7% (Business Insurance, August 4, 2026).

Claims adjusting. Revenue at independent adjusting firms rises and falls with weather claims, and 2026 has brought fewer of them. Crawford & Company's revenues before reimbursements were $321.4 million in the second quarter, down 0.5%, and its United States property and casualty revenue fell 10.2% on fewer weather-related claims, while its adjusted EBITDA rose 20% (Crawford, August 3, 2026). Buyers of adjusting firms therefore place the most value on daily claims and third-party administration revenue, which continues in years without a major storm.
