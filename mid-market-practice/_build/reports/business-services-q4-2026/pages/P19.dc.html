Staffing revenue is growing again after three years of decline. Staffing Industry Analysts' August 2026 Pulse Survey recorded median year-on-year revenue growth of 5% across 128 firms, the highest reading since late 2022, led by engineering at 10% and industrial at 8% (SIA, September 29, 2026). The American Staffing Association's index for the second week of September stood 3.3% above the same week of 2025 (ASA, September 22, 2026). The recovery follows a deep decline: ASA measured 2025 staffing sales at $113.5 billion, down 8.5% (ASA, March 30, 2026), and temporary help employment of 2.49 million in September 2026 remains 21% below its March 2022 peak of 3.16 million (BLS data via FRED).

The segments are recovering at different speeds, and a buyer will value a firm according to which one it serves.

Public company results for the second quarter confirm the pattern. TrueBlue's revenue rose 12% with its on-demand PeopleReady business up 23% (TrueBlue, August 4, 2026), ManpowerGroup's US revenue grew 6.0% (ManpowerGroup, Form 8-K), Kforce posted a third consecutive quarter of growth at 4.5% (Kforce) and Korn Ferry's fee revenue rose 7% in its quarter ended July 31 (Korn Ferry, September 9, 2026). Robert Half's revenue was still 2% lower, although its chief executive reported that hiring demand continues to improve (Robert Half, July 23, 2026). The PEOs are the exception: TriNet's average worksite employees fell 11% (TriNet, July 30, 2026) and Barrett Business Services named soft client hiring as its primary headwind (BBSI, August 5, 2026).
