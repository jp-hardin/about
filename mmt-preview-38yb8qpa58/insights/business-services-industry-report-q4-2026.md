## 2. Market Conditions

Six developments across the five sectors, fourth quarter 2026

Across commercial and facility, marketing, engineering, financial and insurance, and staffing services, the quarter's developments point the same way: acquirers are paying for revenue that repeats under contract and for licensed or tenured people, while costlier debt makes documented earnings the price of entry. Completed Middle Market transactions averaged 7.0x EBITDA in the second quarter (GF Data via ACG), the average multiple on sales of $5 million to $50 million reached 5.8x, and 87% of sales above $5 million drew three or more offers (IBBA and M&A Source).

- **Fire and Life Safety.** Pye-Barker Fire & Safety completed 22 acquisitions in the first half of 2026 after 57 in 2025 (Pye-Barker), and APi Group grew 10.1% organically in the second quarter with a backlog above $5 billion (APi Group). Acquirers compete most actively for contracted inspection and service work.
- **Rates and Leverage.** The Federal Reserve raised its target range to 3.75% to 4.00% on September 16 (Federal Reserve), and the 10-year Treasury yield averaged 4.99% in September (FRED). Average total debt on platform acquisitions fell to 2.9x EBITDA from 3.4x, which puts a premium on documented earnings and contracted customers (GF Data via ACG).
- **Engineering Backlog.** The median engineering firm holds 12 months of backlog, and 88% of firms report at least one open position (ACEC). Bernhard Capital agreed to buy Bowman for about $1.0 billion of enterprise value, a 58% premium, as buyers of engineering firms pay for licensed staff and funded backlog (Bowman).
- **Commercial Insurance Pricing.** Commercial premiums fell 2% on average in the second quarter, with property down 6.3%, while small commercial and casualty accounts are seeing the least pricing pressure (Insurance Journal). Aon agreed on August 31 to buy USI from KKR for $17 billion including debt (Insurance Journal).
- **Staffing Recovery.** Median staffing revenue grew 5% in August, the fastest growth since late 2022 (Staffing Industry Analysts), although temporary help employment remains 21% below its March 2022 peak (BLS via FRED). Skilled trades, engineering, and healthcare placements are drawing the acquirers.
- **Advertising and AI.** US advertising revenue is forecast to grow 9.6% in 2026 (MediaPost composite), and marketing budgets hold at 7.8% of company revenue, with 15.3% of those budgets directed to AI (Gartner via Marketing Dive). Retained clients, first-party data, and measurable outcomes hold their value.

## 3. Our View

Expectations for the fourth quarter and the first half of 2027

Global M&A fell 41% from the second quarter on the rate move, but the decline sits in the largest transactions, and three-quarters of buyouts are add-ons financed off platform facilities and sponsor equity committed before the Fed hike. Through the first half of 2027 we expect GF Data's averages to drift toward the mid-6x range as leverage keeps compressing, while business services transactions under $50 million hold between 5.5x and 6.0x, because the buyers are consolidators pricing route density and contract renewals rather than the cost of debt.

- **Structure Before Price.** With platform leverage at 2.9x and senior debt priced at 7.8%, buyers close the gap with rollover equity, earnouts, and seller notes rather than lower headline numbers. Cash at close, now 83% to 92%, moves toward the low end of that range in 2027, and the owners who do best decide early how much deferred consideration they will accept and on what metric.
- **Market Timing.** With jobs growth at 66,000 a month, unemployment at 4.2%, and engineering executives putting recession odds at 41%, an owner with a renewal book or funded backlog has a better story in the first quarter of 2027 than after a softer year shows up in trailing figures, which argues for preparing now and going to market early in the new year.
- **Commercial and Facility.** Fire and life safety stays the most contested corner of business services, and the premium narrows to inspection-led, contracted revenue with company-held licenses, which is what Pye-Barker, APi, and AI Fire are paying for.
- **Marketing and Agencies.** The 9.6% advertising forecast masks a repricing inside agencies: buyers pay for retained vertical specialists, proprietary data, and measurable outcomes, while labor-hour billing models see multiple compression as AI takes the production work.
- **Engineering and Consulting.** Bernhard taking Bowman private at roughly 22x reported EBITDA and a 58% premium, while the listed group trades at 15x, shows private capital valuing licensed staff and a year of backlog well above the public market. Founder-owned firms with power, water, and data center backlog have an open window; federally exposed firms do not.
- **Insurance and Staffing.** Softening premiums slow organic growth, so aggregators lean harder on acquisitions, Aon's purchase of USI restarts large-cap consolidation, and agency prices hold with more consideration tied to retention. The staffing recovery is concentrated in engineering, industrial, and healthcare placements, so specialty firms should prepare to sell into improving comparables in 2027 and generalist commercial staffing should wait.

## 4. Sector by Sector

### Five Sectors

Buyers, drivers, and valuation tests by sector

Each of the five sectors has its own consolidators, but buyers apply the same five tests across all of them: revenue that repeats under contract through service agreements, retainers, and renewals; licensed, certified, or tenured people whose credentials the company holds; management beyond the owner that holds the client relationships; no client above a fifth of revenue; and margins that held through the downturn, as proof that pricing and staffing can adjust. The active buyers named are drawn from transactions announced between October 2025 and October 2026.

- **Commercial and Facility.** Recurring monthly revenue at the 100 largest security dealers rose 7% to $740 million in 2025 (SDM), and SGS grew 5.6% organically in the first half (SGS). Pye-Barker, APi Group, AI Fire, Cintas, Allied Universal, DEX Imaging, UBEO, SGS, and Phenna Group reward annually renewing inspection, monitoring, and service contracts and company-held licenses.
- **Marketing and Tech-Enabled.** US internet advertising revenue reached $294.6 billion in 2025, up 13.9% (IAB and PwC), and North American promotional products sales reached a record $27.7 billion (ASI). Sponsor-backed platforms such as OneMagnify, Power Digital, and Wpromote buy alongside Publicis, Havas, Accenture, Cognizant, HALO, and Proforma, paying for retained clients, vertical depth, proprietary data, and a team beyond the founder.
- **Engineering and Architecture.** The 500 largest design firms reported $158.7 billion of revenue, up 7.4% (ENR), and 447 transactions were announced among AEC firms in 2025 (PSMJ). Bernhard Capital Partners, Ardurra (owned by Littlejohn & Co.), Salas O'Brien, SAM Companies, Pape-Dawson, and J.S. Held reward funded backlog of a year or more, licensed staff, and a second generation of principals.
- **Financial and Insurance.** Managing general agents wrote $108.7 billion of direct premium in 2025, up 17.8% (AM Best via Risk & Insurance), and advisory firm sales moved $342.9 billion of client assets in the first half (WealthManagement.com). Aon, Gallagher, Inszone, EPIC, King Risk Partners, World Insurance Associates, Hightower, and Mercer Advisors pay for recurring, high-retention commissions and fees and producers under contract beyond the owner.
- **Staffing and Human Capital.** US temporary and contract staffing sales were $113.5 billion in 2025, down 8.5% (ASA), but industrial staffing revenue is now forecast to grow 7% in 2026 (Staffing Industry Analysts). Korn Ferry, Aerotek, ALKU, CHG Healthcare, Airswift, and sponsor-backed specialists reward specialty placements, gross margin held through the downturn, and direct client relationships.

### Fire and Life Safety

Why inspection and service revenue draws acquirers

Fire and life safety work is contracted or mandated, and inspection, testing, and monitoring repeat on a schedule, so revenue continues when new construction slows; a buyer that already runs routes in a region can serve an acquired book of accounts with the technicians it has. Pye-Barker alone bought 79 fire and security companies in the eighteen months to June 2026, with APi Group, Sciens Building Solutions, and AI Fire buying alongside it.

- **Acquisition Pace.** Pye-Barker completed 22 acquisitions in the first half of 2026 after 57 in 2025 (Pye-Barker), and its recent purchases include Union AlarmTronic, a New Jersey alarm, access control, and video company, in September 2026 and Suppression Systems, Inc. in the Pacific Northwest in August 2026.
- **Inspection Leads.** APi Group bought CertaSite, a Midwest inspection business with about $90 million of revenue, because it leads with inspections and earns repair and service work from them. APi aims to earn 60% of revenue from inspection, service, and monitoring, and it grew 10.1% organically in the second quarter with backlog above $5 billion (APi Group).
- **Security and Guarding.** Recurring monthly revenue of the 100 largest security dealers reached $740M in 2025, up 7% (SDM). Allied Universal closed on Aegis Protective Services and Admiral Security Services in June 2026, and Sciens Building Solutions bought Southern Fire Control, a Florida sprinkler contractor, in January 2026; purchase prices were not disclosed.
- **Listed Multiples.** Enterprise value to trailing EBITDA runs from 28.1x at Cintas, 19.8x at Rollins (a pest control company included as a route-based comparison), and 18.8x at APi Group down to 14.6x at FirstService, 10.4x at ABM Industries, and 5.9x at BrightView, which depend more heavily on labor hours (StockAnalysis).
- **What Buyers Ask.** An owner should expect to be asked what share of revenue comes from inspection, monitoring, and service agreements, whether the licenses are held by the company or by the owner personally, and how long the technicians have been with the business.

Three fire protection businesses sold to operators already in the field

Three fire protection businesses sold to two buyers that already operate in the field: Cintas, a listed company serving more than one million businesses over approximately 12,500 local delivery routes (Cintas annual report), and AI Fire, a private equity-backed fire and life safety platform that has been adding regional contractors and made two of the three purchases. ### Recent Transactions

Six transactions announced between June and October 2026, one or more from each sector

The transactions announced since June show acquirers in each sector paying for the same attributes: monitoring revenue, distribution scale, licensed staff and funded backlog, multi-generation client books, skilled trades tied to construction demand, and long-term outsourcing contracts. Two disclosed prices frame the group, with Bernhard Capital agreeing to pay about $1.0 billion for Bowman and Korn Ferry agreeing to pay about 11 times EBITDA for AMS.

- **Alarm and Monitoring.** Pye-Barker Engineered Solutions added Union AlarmTronic, a second-generation New Jersey provider of alarm, access control, and video systems, in September 2026, following 22 acquisitions by Pye-Barker in the first half of 2026 and 57 in 2025 (Pye-Barker). Founder-led alarm and fire companies with monitoring revenue continue to find a buyer quickly.
- **Promotional Products.** Zebra Marketing agreed in October 2026 to acquire East Beach Specialties, a promotional products distributor on the Mississippi Gulf Coast (ASI). Industry sales grew 2.5% in the second quarter while firms under $1 million of revenue were essentially flat (ASI Central), and scale is separating distributors as smaller firms join larger ones.
- **Engineering Take-Private.** Bernhard Capital Partners agreed in August 2026 to buy Bowman, a founder-led engineering firm with more than 2,500 employees and a backlog of $658.7 million, up 50.3% (Bowman results), for about $1.0 billion; the $43.00 per share offer is a 58% premium to the prior close (Bowman).
- **Multi-Generation Agencies.** Inszone Insurance Services acquired Harris Insurance Services, founded in 1925 in West Tulsa, Oklahoma, in August 2026 (Insurance Business), while King Risk Partners, EPIC, and World Insurance Associates each bought a long-established agency in the same period (Insurance Journal). Multi-generation agencies are selling to consolidators while premium rates soften.
- **Data Center Staffing.** VeriWorks entered data center construction staffing in September 2026 by acquiring LaborWorx, which supplies skilled trades for data center construction (Staffing Industry Analysts). In September the 2026 forecast for industrial staffing revenue was raised to growth of 7% from 1% (SIA forecast).
- **Outsourcing Contracts.** Korn Ferry agreed in June 2026 to buy AMS for £850 million, roughly $1.1 billion, against about $650 million of fee revenue and about $100 million of run-rate adjusted EBITDA (Korn Ferry). The long-term outsourcing contracts earned a multiple in line with the 10.0x median of far larger listed staffing companies.

Because listed multiples describe companies many times larger than a founder-owned business and use reported EBITDA, the private-deal reference points matter more to an owner: 7.0x for completed Middle Market transactions in the second quarter of 2026 (GF Data via ACG), 5.8x for transactions of $5 million to $50 million, and 4.0x for $2 million to $5 million (IBBA and M&A Source).

### Operating and Valuation Metrics

Sector medians, trailing twelve months

Among the listed companies, revenue multiples follow margin: financial and insurance services pair the widest EBITDA margin with the highest multiple of revenue, while staffing, where most revenue passes through to payroll, sits at the bottom on both measures. An owner should therefore compare a business with its own sector rather than with business services as a whole, keeping in mind that reported EBITDA is lower than the adjusted EBITDA companies present (StockAnalysis).

- **Revenue Growth.** Median trailing revenue growth, including acquisitions, was highest in financial and insurance at 14.3%, reflecting large acquisitions by the listed brokers and CBIZ, and in engineering and architecture at 11.8%, supported by backlog; commercial and facility grew 5.2%, staffing 4.1%, and marketing, sales, and tech 3.9% (StockAnalysis).
- **EBITDA Margin.** Median EBITDA margin runs from 27.9% in financial and insurance and 18.1% in commercial and facility to 15.8% in marketing, sales, and tech, 10.9% in engineering and architecture, and 3.5% in staffing, where most revenue passes through to payroll (StockAnalysis).
- **Free Cash Flow.** Median free cash flow margin is 15.6% in financial and insurance, 12.4% in commercial and facility, 10.2% in marketing, sales, and tech, 6.5% in engineering and architecture, and 2.8% in staffing, so commercial and facility services converts more revenue to cash than engineering services (StockAnalysis).
- **Revenue Multiples.** Median enterprise value to trailing revenue is 3.93x in financial and insurance, 2.41x in commercial and facility, 1.58x in engineering and architecture, 1.25x in marketing, sales, and tech, and 0.55x in staffing (StockAnalysis).

## 7. Indicators to Watch

Growth, hiring, rates, and services employment through September 2026

The economy is growing at a moderate pace and hiring has slowed, while long-term borrowing costs have moved higher than a year ago. Inside business services, employment trends differ by sector: architecture and engineering payrolls are still rising, building services employment has been flat since 2024, and temporary help remains well below its 2022 peak. Recent months are preliminary.

- **Real GDP.** The economy grew at a 2.2% annual rate in the second quarter of 2026 after 2.5% in the first; since the third quarter of 2021, quarterly growth has ranged from 7% in the fourth quarter of 2021 to -1% in the first quarter of 2022 (Bureau of Economic Analysis via FRED).
- **Unemployment Rate.** Unemployment stood at 4.2% in September 2026, compared with 4.4% a year earlier and a recent high of 4.5% in November 2025; the low over the period was 3.4% in April 2023 (Bureau of Labor Statistics via FRED).
- **Payroll Employment.** Employers added 29,000 jobs in September, and the average over the past six months was 66,000, a pace that includes a loss of 156,000 jobs in February 2026 and of 10,000 in July 2026 (Bureau of Labor Statistics via FRED).
- **Treasury and Fed Funds.** The 10-year Treasury yield averaged 4.99% in September, up from 4.12% a year earlier, while the federal funds rate averaged 3.75% (Federal Reserve via FRED).
- **Temporary Help.** Temporary help employment of 2.49 million is 21% below its March 2022 peak and slightly above its level a year earlier, after reaching its low for the period in December 2025 (Bureau of Labor Statistics via FRED).
- **Engineering and Building Services.** Architecture and engineering employment is 14.6% above its September 2021 level, while building services employment is 7.3% above and has changed little since 2024 (Bureau of Labor Statistics via FRED).

## 8. What We're Reading

Six sources from the third quarter

The quarter's sources cover engineering backlog, the staffing recovery, commercial insurance pricing, the global deal market, the Federal Reserve's rate decision, and small-company deal multiples. Read together, they describe a lower Middle Market that remains competitive for well-prepared businesses even as large-deal volume falls and lenders advance less debt against each dollar of earnings.

- **ACEC Research Institute.** The third-quarter survey of engineering firms, published August 27, found a median backlog of 12 months, the strongest sentiment in data centers and in water and wastewater, recession odds of 41% within six months, and open positions at 88% of firms; backlog and a bench of licensed engineers are the first two facts a buyer will test.
- **Staffing Industry Analysts.** The August Pulse Survey of 128 staffing firms, published September 29, recorded median revenue growth of 5%, led by engineering staffing at 10% and industrial staffing at 8% after three years of decline; the recovery is strongest in the skilled categories where this year's acquisitions are concentrated.
- **Insurance Journal.** The Council of Insurance Agents & Brokers recorded an average second-quarter commercial premium decline of 2%, with large accounts down 3.7%, small accounts down 0.5%, and commercial property down 6.3% while umbrella and commercial auto rose; account size and line of business now explain most of the difference in agency growth.
- **Reuters via Investing.com.** Announced global M&A fell 41% from the second quarter to $993 billion in the third quarter, although year-to-date value of $3.9 trillion is the highest for the period since 2001 (LSEG data); the slowdown is concentrated in the largest transactions, and add-ons made up about three-quarters of second-quarter buyouts by count (PitchBook).
- **Federal Reserve.** On September 16 the Federal Open Market Committee raised the federal funds target range by 25 basis points to 3.75% to 4.00% and stated that inflation remains elevated; senior debt on Middle Market acquisitions was already priced at 7.8%, and average total debt on platform deals stood at 2.9x EBITDA (GF Data via ACG).
- **IBBA and M&A Source.** The second-quarter Market Pulse put the average multiple on sales of $5 million to $50 million at 5.8x EBITDA, up from 5.5x a year earlier and the highest since the first quarter of 2022, with 83% to 92% of the price paid in cash at closing and three or more offers on 87% of sales above $5 million.
