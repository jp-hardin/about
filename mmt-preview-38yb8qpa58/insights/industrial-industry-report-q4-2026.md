Benchmark International's Middle Market team. October 2026 | Jared Hardin, Managing Director.

American manufacturing has expanded for nine consecutive months. Borrowing costs rose in September, and buyers have become more selective about what they pay for.

## 2. Market Conditions

### The Quarter in Brief

Demand spans the five sectors, and buyers are acquiring through existing platforms. Higher financing costs in September reduced sponsors’ borrowing capacity.

Strategic acquirers and sponsor platforms continue to buy machining, distribution, contracting, environmental, and logistics businesses for capabilities already in place, and they pay the most for companies tied to power, data centers, aerospace, and service work that codes and regulations require.

Financing has become the main constraint on price. After the Federal Reserve raised its target range to 3.75% to 4.00% on September 16, a sponsor can borrow less against each acquisition and either bids less or moves more of the price into earn-outs, while strategic buyers are affected less (Federal Reserve).

- **Manufacturing Demand.** The ISM Manufacturing PMI was 54.5 in September, its ninth consecutive month of expansion, and manufacturing technology orders reached $4.03 billion in the first seven months of the year, 37.1% ahead of 2025 (ISM; AMT data reported by MDM). With capacity utilization at 75.9%, the scarce inputs are skilled labor and certified capacity rather than floor space (Federal Reserve G.17).
- **Platform Add-Ons.** Add-on acquisitions made up about three-quarters of all buyouts in the second quarter, when private equity held 13,509 US companies (PitchBook). Pye-Barker Fire & Safety completed 22 acquisitions in the first half alone, so the sponsor across the table from a founder-owned industrial company is usually an operating platform that already knows its trade (Pye-Barker).
- **Power and Data Centers.** Quanta Services ended June with backlog of $53.4 billion against $35.8 billion a year earlier (Quanta Services). Public infrastructure funding is less certain, because the surface transportation authorization was extended only through December 11 under the continuing resolution signed in September (AASHTO Journal).
- **Recurring Service Revenue.** APi Group is working toward 60% of revenue from inspection and service, and Transcat's service revenue grew 27% (APi Group; Transcat). Buyers pay for service, repair, inspection, and aftermarket parts revenue when an owner reports it separately from project and product sales, with the renewal history behind it.
- **Selective Financing.** Global M&A value fell 41% from the second quarter to $993 billion in the third (LSEG data reported by Reuters). GF Data's average multiple eased to 7.0 times trailing adjusted EBITDA from 7.3 times, and debt on platform acquisitions fell to 2.9 times from 3.4 times (GF Data via ACG).

### Power Demand: How the Buildout Reaches the Industrial Sectors

Electricity demand grows about 2% a year and the equipment is sold out years ahead, so acquirers buy the people, permits, and delivery slots already in place.

**224 GW**

NERC 10-year forecast of summer peak demand growth, raised from 132

**116 GW**

GE Vernova gas turbine backlog and slot reservations

**160 weeks**

Lead time for generator step-up transformers

**9.9 months**

Backlog at contractors doing data center work

The Energy Information Administration expects US electricity sales to grow about 2% in 2026 and again in 2027 on data center development and commercial and industrial activity (EIA), and NERC raised its 10-year forecast of summer peak demand growth to 224 gigawatts from 132 (POWER Magazine).

Because turbines, transformers, and switchgear are committed years ahead, acquirers in each of the five Industrials sectors are paying for production, inventory, crews, and delivery positions that already exist. A buyer will ask how much backlog is tied to funded power and data center work and whether the technicians are in place to perform it at the margins earned so far.

- **Power as the Constraint.** Greg Abel of Berkshire Hathaway, Toby Rice of EQT, and Satya Nadella of Microsoft each named power generation, and the ability to build close to it, as the limit on data center growth. Meta alone expects capital expenditures of $130 billion to $145 billion in 2026 (Meta).
- **Equipment Backlogs.** GE Vernova's gas turbine backlog and slot reservations rose to 116 gigawatts from 100 during the second quarter, and lead times for generator step-up transformers reached 160 weeks in the first quarter (GE Vernova; Reuters, citing Wood Mackenzie). Generac's commercial and industrial sales grew 29% and its data center backlog reached about $1.6 billion (Generac).
- **Switchgear and Transformers.** nVent agreed to buy Maverick Power, a Texas maker of switchgear and modular power for data centers, for $1.75 billion plus up to $550 million, 11.5 times anticipated 2026 adjusted EBITDA on the upfront payment (nVent). Mission Critical Group added a Texas switchgear maker and CORE, which keeps more than 1,000 transformers in inventory and delivers custom units in as little as 16 weeks.
- **Industrial Services.** EMCOR's US Industrial Services business grew 25.9% in the second quarter (EMCOR). It serves refining, petrochemical, utility, and power customers, where turnaround and maintenance work recurs on a schedule that an acquirer can underwrite.
- **Contractor Backlog.** Contractors doing data center work reported 9.9 months of backlog against 8.3 months for those that are not (ABC). Limbach lowered its margin guidance after gross margin fell to 21.5% from 28.0%, mainly on an acquired business, with competition for skilled labor and materials in data center markets as a second factor (Limbach).
- **Heavy Haul and Rigging.** Data center construction spending is up more than 73% over the past year (ABC, reported by Construction Dive), and the transformers, switchgear, and generators behind it move by heavy haul and project freight. JK Technology Services bought 360 Rigging, a Manassas, Virginia crane and rigging company that installs equipment in data centers (Vertikal).

## 3. Our View

### Our View

Five conclusions from the quarter, and what they mean for an owner planning a sale in 2027.

Buyers are paying premiums for specialized capabilities, and higher borrowing costs affect the offers they can make. The five conclusions below are the views of the Benchmark International Middle Market team as of October 19, 2026, and they are opinions; the price of any company depends on that business.

For an owner going to market in 2027, Benchmark International advises reporting service, inspection, and aftermarket revenue separately from project and product sales, with renewal history; showing backlog by end market and funded status and naming the technicians who will perform it; and documenting how steel, diesel, and transformer cost increases pass through each contract.

- **Specialized Capabilities.** nVent paid 11.5 times for switchgear it could not build in time and Clean Harbors about 17 times standalone EBITDA for a route network. A founder-owned company holding a certification, permit, delivery slot, or license that takes years to replicate is priced on that asset, at 8 to 12 times, against about 6 times for project and product capacity.
- **Sponsor Financing.** With the funds rate at 3.75% to 4.00%, the 10-year near 5%, and sponsor leverage at 2.9 times, a platform bid carries half a turn less debt than it did in the spring. Benchmark International expects the strategic share of Middle Market industrial sales, 56% of its closed transactions with a classified buyer over two years, to rise through 2027, and sponsor offers to lean on earn-outs and seller notes.
- **Data Center Contractors.** Listed building products and construction companies grew revenue 22% while total construction spending fell 1.7% and manufacturing construction fell 19%. A Middle Market contractor with funded data center backlog carries a premium, one without it competes in a shrinking market, and Limbach's margin reset means buyers will underwrite the crews along with the backlog.
- **Recurring Service Revenue.** APi Group is steering toward 60% inspection and service revenue and Transcat's service line grew 27%, while a project contractor such as 1901 Inc. changed hands at 5.7 times. Buyers pay for revenue that renews under codes, regulations, and maintenance schedules, and an owner who reports service, repair, inspection, and parts separately sells a different company from one reporting a single revenue line.
- **Freight and Border Services.** Cass shipments are up 2.1% off a two-year low, transportation shares rose a median 39%, and C.H. Robinson agreed to pay $5.8 billion for RXO with $300 million of synergies behind it. With diesel at $6.29 a gallon and US-Mexico trade at a record $94.8 billion a month, customs licensing, bonded warehousing, and cross-border capability will be priced above asset-based capacity through 2027.

### Our View: Sector Outlook

Demand for owner-operated businesses differs across the five sectors.

Benchmark International rates buyer demand in each sector by weighing listed-company growth, valuation, and cash conversion against its own closings in the sector, on a scale of one to five. Energy, Power & Environmental rates Strongest; Building & Construction and Manufacturing & Industrial Tech rate Strong; Distribution & Services rates Steady; and Transportation & Logistics rates Mixed.

The ratings come with three steps the firm advises owners to take, each tied to the change in sponsor financing and the prices buyers have paid for hard-to-replicate assets.

- **Energy, Power & Environmental.** The sector is rated Strongest, the highest of the five ratings, on transformer lead times above 160 weeks and 21.2% EBITDA growth, offset by 2.5x leverage.
- **Building and Manufacturing.** Building & Construction is rated Strong on EBITDA up 28% on 21.8% revenue growth and 45 Benchmark International closings. Manufacturing & Industrial Tech is rated Strong with the highest margin at 22.1% and the highest multiple at 17.3x.
- **Distribution and Transportation.** Distribution & Services is rated Steady at 17.0x against 13.1x at year-end 2025, with margins near 11%. Transportation & Logistics is rated Mixed, with the lowest multiple at 12.2x and EBITDA down 0.8% despite shares up 38.9%.
- **Compare Bids on Proceeds.** Sponsor platform leverage fell to 2.9x EBITDA from 3.4x, so owners should compare offers on cash at close, earn-out, and seller note terms rather than on headline multiple alone.
- **Invite Strategic Buyers.** Strategic acquirers bought 56% of the companies with a classified buyer in Benchmark International's closed transactions, so a sale process should include them alongside sponsor platforms.
- **Document Licenses and Slots.** Licenses, certifications, permits, and delivery slots belong in the first management presentation, because buyers have paid 8x to 12x for them against about 6x for businesses without them.

## 4. Sector by Sector

The five Industrials sectors differ in margin, valuation, growth, and the buyers they attract. Manufacturing & Industrial Tech earns the highest EBITDA margin at 22.1% and trades at the highest multiple at 17.3x, Building Products & Construction grew revenue 21.8% and accounts for 45 Benchmark International closings, the most of any sector, and Transportation & Logistics trades at the lowest multiple, 12.2x, even after shares rose 38.9% over the year.

- **Manufacturing & Industrial Tech.** The sector pairs a 22.1% EBITDA margin and a 17.3x multiple with 8.8% revenue growth and 22 Benchmark International deals. Active buyers include nVent, Franklin Electric, Precinmac (Centerbridge Partners), DXP Enterprises, Cemtrex, and Assessimus Group; listed references are Parker Hannifin, Rockwell Automation, ITT, Flowserve, Franklin Electric, Graco, Lincoln Electric, and Mueller Industries.
- **Industrial Distribution & Services.** Distributors trade at 17.0x EBITDA against 13.1x at year-end 2025, with an 11.2% margin, and shares of all eight listed distributors rose over the year. Grainger, Applied Industrial, MSC Industrial, DXP Enterprises, Distribution Solutions, WESCO, and Global Industrial.
- **Building Products & Construction.** Listed contractors grew revenue 21.8% and EBITDA 28% and trade at 15.8x. - **Energy, Power & Environmental.** The sector carries the heaviest leverage at 2.5x net debt to EBITDA and the lowest free cash flow conversion at 44.7%, while EBITDA grew 21.2%. ## 5. Deals and Buyers

### Featured Deal: The Carmi Companies

A Laredo logistics and customs brokerage group and a Mexico manufacturing services company have combined to form an end-to-end cross-border platform.

Benchmark International advised The Carmi Companies, a Laredo, Texas logistics group founded in 1983, on its combination with The Nearshore Company, announced on March 30, 2026. Pairing Carmi's freight coordination, customs brokerage, warehousing, and transloading with Nearshore's Mexico manufacturing facilities and employer-of-record services forms a single cross-border platform.

Buyers price Carmi's brokerage licenses, bonded warehousing, and four decades of Laredo relationships as the capability; trucks are capacity, which buyers can rent.

- **The Carmi Companies.** Founded in 1983 in Laredo, Texas, Carmi is a single-source logistics group that coordinates land, air, and ocean freight and provides customs brokerage, warehousing, and transloading.
- **The Nearshore Company.** Founded in 1992 in Brownsville, Texas, The Nearshore Company offers manufacturing facilities in Mexico together with employer-of-record services, which the combination pairs with Carmi's logistics.
- **Record Mexico Trade.** Trade with Mexico reached a monthly record of $94.8 billion in July, 27.5% above a year earlier, and $36.95 billion of it moved through Laredo (Census data reported by FreightWaves).
- **Border Broker Acquisitions.** Logistics platforms have responded by buying brokers at the border, and Redwood Logistics and Imperative Logistics Group each acquired a Laredo customs broker this year (FreightWaves). Gaurang Shastri of Lincoln International described the market as one in which buyers acquire capabilities rather than capacity.

### Recent Transactions

Public acquirers disclosed prices of about 6 to 10 times EBITDA, and sponsor-owned platforms bought family-owned companies on undisclosed terms.

Ten transactions, two in each of the five sectors, show where buyers are paying. Public acquirers that disclosed terms paid from about 5.7 times expected EBITDA for a project contractor to about 10 times trailing adjusted EBITDA for a flow control distributor, and Clean Harbors paid about 17 times standalone EBITDA for route density.

Sponsor-owned platforms bought family- and founder-owned machining, industrial gas, transformer, and customs brokerage companies on undisclosed terms. In power, environmental services, and logistics, buyers paid for technicians, permits, equipment supply, and border capability they did not already have.

- **Franklin Electric and Cat Pumps.** Franklin Electric acquired Cat Pumps, a Minneapolis maker of high-pressure pumps, for $350 million in cash plus up to $50 million in stock, about 7.8 times 2025 adjusted EBITDA of $45 million on $115 million of revenue, a margin near 39%, for pumps specified into customers' equipment (Franklin Electric).
- **Ferguson and FloWorks.** Ferguson bought FloWorks, a flow control distributor with about $1 billion of revenue, from Wynnchurch Capital for $1.6 billion, a price it described as about 10 times trailing adjusted EBITDA after including roughly $45 million of expected synergies (MDM).
- **Limbach and 1901 Inc.** Limbach paid $63 million plus an earn-out of up to $6 million for 1901 Inc., a Madison, Wisconsin mechanical, electrical, and plumbing contractor. The upfront payment is about 5.7 times the $11 million of 2027 adjusted EBITDA Limbach expects, a 7.9% margin on $140 million of revenue and the profile of a project contractor (Pulse 2.0).
- **Clean Harbors and EnviroServe.** Clean Harbors agreed to pay One Rock Capital Partners $470 million for EnviroServe, with 40 locations, about $250 million of revenue, and $27 million of adjusted EBITDA: about nine times after synergies, or about 17 times standalone, a month after agreeing to 8.7 times EBITDA after synergies for ES&H (Waste Advantage; Clean Harbors).
- **C.H. Robinson and RXO.** C.H. Robinson agreed on October 5 to acquire RXO, a truck brokerage, managed transportation, and last-mile provider, at an implied value of $5.8 billion, a 29% premium, with about $300 million of expected annual cost savings supporting a price at the top of the brokerage market (C.H. Robinson and RXO).
- **Platform Add-Ons.** Precinmac (Centerbridge Partners) bought Advance Mfg. Co., family-owned since 1961; Meritus Gas Partners (AEA Investors) made Metroplex Welding Supply its third 2026 acquisition; Mission Critical Group (Emerald Lake Capital Management) bought CORE Transformers; Redwood Logistics (AEA Investors) bought Laredo broker EELCO; and Bay Crane bought Clark Rigging & Rental, all on undisclosed terms.

- **Manufacturing & Industrial Tech.** The median rose from a low of 12.8x in 2022 to a five-year high of 17.8x at year-end 2025 and stands at 17.3x today, 2.1x above the five-year median of 15.2x (StockAnalysis).
- **Distribution & Services.** From a low of 9.9x in 2022 the median recovered to 13.1x at year-end 2025 and now reads 17.0x, above the five-year high of 15.0x set in 2021 and 3.9x above the five-year median (StockAnalysis).
- **Building Products & Construction.** The sector peaked at 17.9x at year-end 2025, up from 10.9x in 2022, and has eased to 15.8x, 0.7x above its five-year median of 15.1x (StockAnalysis).
- **Energy, Power & Environmental.** The median reached 18.0x in 2024 and has since settled to 16.6x at year-end 2025 and 16.3x today, 0.1x below the five-year median of 16.4x (StockAnalysis).
- **Transportation & Logistics.** At 12.2x the sector is the lowest of the five yet matches its five-year high, set in 2023, and stands well above the 2022 low of 6.2x and 1.1x above its median of 11.1x (StockAnalysis).

### Operating Metrics

Listed contractors grew revenue about 22% while total construction spending fell. Manufacturers reported the highest margins.

Among the 40 listed companies, Building Products & Construction led median revenue growth at +21.8% and EBITDA growth at +28.0%, while Manufacturing & Industrial Tech earned the highest median EBITDA margin at 22.1%. Transportation & Logistics was the only sector with lower EBITDA, at −0.8%, though it converted the largest share of EBITDA into free cash flow.

Revenue growth includes acquisitions, and the figures use reported EBITDA, which is lower than the adjusted EBITDA companies present (StockAnalysis).

- **Revenue Growth.** Trailing twelve-month revenue grew a median +21.8% in Building Products & Construction, +8.8% in Manufacturing & Industrial Tech, +8.6% in Distribution & Services, +5.4% in Energy, Power & Environmental, and +4.1% in Transportation & Logistics (StockAnalysis).
- **EBITDA Growth.** Latest fiscal-year EBITDA rose a median +28.0% in Building Products & Construction and +21.2% in Energy, Power & Environmental, against +9.1% in Manufacturing & Industrial Tech, +5.6% in Distribution & Services, and −0.8% in Transportation & Logistics (StockAnalysis).
- **EBITDA Margin.** Manufacturing & Industrial Tech earns a median trailing margin of 22.1% and Energy, Power & Environmental 16.8%, well ahead of Distribution & Services at 11.2%, Building Products & Construction at 10.7%, and Transportation & Logistics at 10.1% (StockAnalysis).
- **Cash Conversion.** Free cash flow as a share of EBITDA is highest in Transportation & Logistics at 65%, followed by Building Products & Construction at 63% and Manufacturing & Industrial Tech at 62%, with Distribution & Services at 50% and Energy, Power & Environmental lowest at 45% (StockAnalysis).

### Valuation Multiples

Transportation shares rose a median 39% in a year and still trade at the lowest multiple of EBITDA of the five sectors.

Transportation shares rose a median 38.9% over 52 weeks, with gains at all 7 companies, yet the sector trades at 12.2x trailing EBITDA, the lowest of the five, against 11.1x at year-end 2025. Distribution re-rated the most, to 17.0x from 13.1x, as shares rose at all 8 distributors, while Building fell to 15.8x from 17.9x and Energy & Environmental carries the most debt at 2.5x net debt to EBITDA (StockAnalysis).

- **Current Multiples.** On share prices from October 9 to 19, 2026, median enterprise value to trailing EBITDA is 17.3x for Manufacturing, 17.0x for Distribution, 16.3x for Energy & Environmental, 15.8x for Building, and 12.2x for Transport (StockAnalysis).
- **Change Since Year-End.** At 2025 fiscal year-ends the medians were 17.9x for Building, 17.8x for Manufacturing, 16.6x for Energy & Environmental, 13.1x for Distribution, and 11.1x for Transport, so Distribution and Transport are the two sectors whose multiples have risen since (StockAnalysis).
- **Share Prices.** Median 52-week share price changes were +38.9% for Transport and +36.1% for Distribution, against +17.9% for Manufacturing, +3.2% for Building, and +2.6% for Energy & Environmental, where shares rose at only 5 of 9 building and 5 of 8 energy companies (StockAnalysis).
- **Net Debt.** Median net debt to trailing EBITDA is 2.5x for Energy & Environmental, 1.3x for Manufacturing, 1.0x for Transport, 0.9x for Distribution, and 0.7x for Building, where three of the nine companies hold more cash than debt (StockAnalysis).

### What Public Companies Said

Revenue growth for the 40 listed companies and what management teams said.

Latest-quarter revenue growth across the 40 listed companies ran from 51.5% at ITT, largely acquisition-driven with organic growth of 12.7%, and 50.0% at Comfort Systems USA down to a 10.7% decline at Primoris Services. Management teams attributed the strongest results to data centers, power, record backlogs, and pricing in capacity-constrained freight.

- **Fastest Growth.** After ITT and Comfort Systems USA, Quanta Services (41.1%), IES Holdings (40.0%), Granite Construction (29.0%), Construction Partners (28.2%), Mueller Industries (25.5%), and Werner (24.0%, its second-quarter figure) grew fastest, a list dominated by construction and power contractors.
- **Data Center Demand.** John Engel said WESCO has posted four consecutive quarters of double-digit sales growth fueled by data centers, and WESCO grew sales 13.0%. Flowserve's Scott Rowe cited demand led by power, nuclear, and energy security investments, though Flowserve reported a 1.6% decline in its latest quarter.
- **Backlog and Services.** Tony Guzzi reported EMCOR's remaining performance obligations at a record level as revenue grew 19.8%, and Clean Harbors Co-CEO Eric Gerstenberg said Technical Services revenue grew 18% on demand for disposal and recycling services, with company revenue up 12%.
- **Distribution and Freight Pricing.** Neil Schrimsher described Applied Industrial's demand recovery as durable and increasingly influenced by structural and secular tailwinds, with revenue up 10.4%. Jim Filter said Schneider sees its strongest pricing where the industry is most capacity constrained, and Schneider grew 10%.
- **Slowest Growth.** Builders FirstSource fell 8.8% and Primoris Services 10.7%, with Primoris noting an accounting review in its filings, while Watsco (2.0%), Graco (3.3%), and Republic Services (4.6%) grew the least among companies reporting gains.

## 7. Indicators to Watch

Demand indicators are rising and so are the costs of serving it: steel is up 23%, diesel is up 68%, and the 10-year Treasury averaged 4.99% in September.

Orders and freight volumes are improving, and the inputs owners must pass through to customers have risen faster. Core capital goods orders reached $87.6B in August, 14.1% above a year earlier, while steel mill product prices rose 23.4% and diesel rose 67.8% to $6.29 a gallon.

Construction is divided: total spending slipped 1.7% to $2.20T and manufacturing construction fell 19.2%. The 10-year Treasury yield averaged 4.99% in September, 0.87 points above a year earlier (FRED).

- **Capital Goods Orders.** Nondefense capital goods orders excluding aircraft reached $87.6B in August 2026, up 14.1% over one year, 17.8% over three years, and 21.4% since October 2021 (US Census Bureau).
- **Capacity Utilization.** Manufacturing capacity utilization was 75.9% in September 2026, 0.2 points above a year earlier, 0.7 points below three years earlier, and 2.1 points below October 2021 (Federal Reserve).
- **Construction Spending.** Total construction spending was $2.20T in August, down 1.7% over one year but 30.5% above October 2021. Manufacturing construction fell 19.2% over one year to $170.7B and 17.3% over three years, though it remains 95.7% above October 2021 (US Census Bureau).
- **Steel and Diesel.** The steel mill product price index reached 381.2 in August, up 23.4% over one year though 12.5% below October 2021 (Bureau of Labor Statistics). Diesel reached $6.29 a gallon at the end of September, 67.8% above a year earlier, after jumping from $3.72 in February (EIA).
- **Freight Volume.** The Cass Freight Index for shipments read 1.038 in August, up 2.1% over one year but down 9.2% over three years and 12.7% below October 2021 (Cass Information Systems).
- **Treasury Yields.** The 10-year Treasury yield averaged 4.99% in September, 0.87 points above a year earlier, 0.61 points above three years earlier, and 3.4 points above October 2021 (Federal Reserve).

## 8. What We're Reading

Four pieces from the quarter that shape how buyers and owners are talking.

Four pieces from the quarter cover separate inputs to a sale process: manufacturing orders, Middle Market financing terms, grid equipment supply, and cross-border freight volume. Each takeaway ties the reading to what an owner should have prepared before going to market, from order trends in the monthly financials to documented delivery slots and lane concentration.

- **ISM Manufacturing PMI.** ISM (October 1, 2026) reported a September PMI of 54.5% with new orders expanding for the ninth consecutive month after four months of contraction, so an owner planning a sale should show two or three quarters of order growth in the monthly financials before going to market.
- **GF Data Financing Terms.** ACG (GF Data, August 27, 2026) reported average purchase multiples slipping to 7.0x from 7.3x across 85 completed second-quarter deals, platform debt down to 2.9x from 3.4x, and senior debt pricing up to 7.8%, so sellers should expect price discipline and use a quality of earnings report to defend a premium.
- **Grid Equipment Supply.** Reuters via Kitco News (July 9, 2026) reported generator step-up transformer lead times past 160 weeks against 143 weeks on average in 2024, with utilities buying equipment for projects five years ahead, so owners serving grid and data center work should document backlog, supplier agreements, and delivery slots before launching a process.
- **Mexico Trade.** FreightWaves (October 7, 2026) reported August US-Mexico trade of $94.3 billion and a 28.5% rise in Laredo two-way trade, so owners with cross-border exposure should document customer mix, tariff pass-through terms, and lane concentration, which buyers test in diligence.
