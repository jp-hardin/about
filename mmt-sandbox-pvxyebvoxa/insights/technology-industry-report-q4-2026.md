## 2. Market Conditions

### Key Trends

Six developments are shaping demand, pricing, and deal activity as the fourth quarter begins.

Demand for AI infrastructure, steady consolidation of managed service providers, and electronics order books that outrun shipments are lifting technology demand, while a higher cost of debt, a paused defense certification mandate, and slower federal awards make buyers more selective about what they pay for.

Buyers pay most readily for contracted, recurring revenue and for capability that customers or regulators require. Completed Middle Market transactions averaged 7.0x EBITDA in the second quarter (GF Data via ACG), and 87% of sales above $5 million drew three or more offers (IBBA and M&A Source).

- **AI Infrastructure Demand.** Gartner expects IT spending of $6.37 trillion in 2026, up 14.2%, with data center systems up 62.5% against 5.3% for IT services (Gartner). Cloud infrastructure revenue grew 43% in the second quarter (Synergy Research Group), and Celestica, TD SYNNEX, and Wesco each reported double-digit growth on data center demand.
- **Managed Service Consolidation.** Buyers announced 64 acquisitions of managed service providers in the first quarter, 73% more than a year earlier, with outside investors leading 80% of them, and Evergreen Services Group alone completed 47 acquisitions in 2025 (Omdia). Evergreen, Thrive, Integris, and The 20 MSP each absorb several providers a year and underwrite contracted managed revenue.
- **Financing Costs.** The Federal Reserve raised its target range to 3.75% to 4.00% on September 16, and the 10-year Treasury yielded 5.24% on October 1 (Federal Reserve; US Treasury). With platform acquisition debt down to 2.9x EBITDA from 3.4x against a 7.0x average price, sponsors fund about 60% of a purchase with equity (GF Data via ACG).
- **Security Spending and CMMC.** Gartner's July 2025 forecast puts 2026 security spending at $240 billion, up 12.5% (Gartner), and cybercrime losses reached $20.9 billion in 2025 (FBI). Because third-party CMMC certification was suspended on July 13 and the review report is unpublished, a Level 2 certificate signals readiness without a contract requirement behind it (Department of War CIO).
- **Hardware Order Books.** North American EMS bookings rose 53.5% in August while shipments rose 1.0%, a three-month book-to-bill ratio of 1.39 (Global Electronics Association). Section 301 duties of 10% to 12.5% took effect July 24 and component prices are up 26.7% in a year, so backlog booked at 2025 prices needs pass-through terms to hold its margin.
- **Federal Technology Budgets.** The fiscal 2027 request restores civilian agency IT to $75.7 billion after a cut to $67.9 billion in fiscal 2026 (FedScoop), but a continuing resolution through December 11 delays new program starts (SpacePolicyOnline). Contract mix sets the price: defense-weighted CACI grew 17.6% while civilian-weighted Booz Allen fell 4.2%.

A technology company that comes to market in the next year will present earnings that passed through three tariff regimes, a 27% rise in component prices, a record copper price, and the first increase in the federal funds rate since 2023. Buyers are rebuilding each of those costs period by period before they settle on the earnings they will pay for.

Exposure depends on the business model: importers of components carry duty exposure, integrators that fix a price on cabling and power work carry copper exposure, and managed service firms with contracted revenue carry mostly financing exposure.

- **Tariffs and Components.** After the Supreme Court held on February 20 that the International Emergency Economic Powers Act does not authorize tariffs, a 10% Section 122 tariff ran until July 24 and Section 301 tariffs of 10% to 12.5% took effect that day. Section 301 duties on Chinese semiconductors remain at 50%, and component prices rose 26.7% in the year to August (Bureau of Labor Statistics via FRED).
- **Copper and Power.** Copper averaged $13,543 a ton in July, up 38.6% from a year earlier, and reached a record $14,875 a ton on the London Metal Exchange on September 10, with a 50% Section 232 tariff on semi-finished copper products. Data center construction costs per megawatt are up 21% since late 2024 and switchgear is up 60% (Cushman & Wakefield).
- **Debt and Earn-Outs.** Total debt on Middle Market platform buyouts fell to 2.9x EBITDA from 3.4x in the second quarter against an average price of 7.0x (GF Data via ACG). Earn-outs appeared in 24% of 2025 transactions, with a median equal to 34% of the closing payment (SRS Acquiom).
- **Rates and Buyer Mix.** The Federal Reserve raised its target range to 3.75% to 4.00% on September 16, and the 10-year Treasury yielded 5.24% on October 1. Because higher rates lower the debt a sponsor can place against the same earnings, price support shifts toward strategic buyers that fund purchases from cash flow.
- **What Buyers Request.** Diligence lists now cover a bill of materials by country of origin with duties paid since February 2025, tariff pass-through terms, contracted and spot pricing for copper-bearing cable and switchgear with the margin effect of a 10% move in copper, and the split of price between cash at closing, contingent payments, and rolled equity.
- **Preparing the Numbers.** An owner who can show landed cost by period, copper recovery by contract, and a clear account of how the business is financed shortens diligence and protects the earnings figure on which the price is built.

Buyers pay full prices for contracted revenue, essential capabilities, and backlog that converts to cash, while lower debt availability makes them more selective elsewhere. At 2.9x debt against a 7.0x average price, about 60% of a typical sponsor purchase is funded with equity (GF Data via ACG), yet 87% of sales above $5 million drew three or more offers (IBBA and M&A Source).

Our advice to owners: document contracted revenue by customer, term, and renewal history, because equity-funded buyers price what they can verify; add pass-through or escalation terms to open backlog before a process, with component and copper prices up more than 25%; and approach strategic buyers in adjacent fields alongside sponsors, since seven of our eleven named sellers sold to one.

- **Managed IT and Cloud Services.** We rate buyer demand Strong. Consolidators announced 64 provider acquisitions in the first quarter and listed IT services trade at 9.7x, and buyers pay more for contracted managed revenue than for project and resale revenue.
- **Cybersecurity.** We rate buyer demand Strong, as strategic buyers pay for security that protects physical operations, shown by the $3.25 billion Dragos valuation. With CMMC certification paused, buyers will credit a Level 2 certificate as readiness and test the self-assessment behind it.
- **Telecom and UCaaS.** We rate buyer demand Steady. Infrastructure funds and adjacent network operators buy local fiber, as TDS showed in New Hampshire, cloud voice grows about 5% a year, and retention and paid AI products, now 13% of ARR at RingCentral, influence value.
- **Systems Integration.** We rate buyer demand Strong: data center construction reached an $85.0 billion annual rate in August, and sponsor-backed platforms such as Pavion and Convergint are paying for tenure and local service density. With copper up 38.6% in a year, buyers will test whether fixed-price backlog carries escalation terms.
- **Electronics and Hardware.** We rate buyer demand Strongest, the strongest seller position in the report, because bookings outpace shipments at a 1.39 book-to-bill and listed manufacturers re-rated to 17.4x. Buyers will diligence backlog conversion and pass-through terms, with component prices up 26.7%.
- **Government Technology.** We rate buyer demand Mixed, since contract mix sets the price: defense-weighted CACI grew 17.6% while civilian-weighted Booz Allen fell 4.2%. The continuing resolution through December 11 delays new starts, so owners should present funded backlog by agency and option period.

## 4. Sector by Sector

Each sector has its own buyers and drivers.

Each of the six technology sectors draws its own set of acquirers and responds to its own demand driver, from recurring managed revenue and outsourced security to fiber builds, integrator backlogs, factory order books, and federal mission budgets.

The active buyers named here come from transactions announced between January 2025 and October 2026, and the listed reference companies form the public peer groups against which the report measures share prices, growth, and multiples.

- **Managed IT and Cloud Services.** Consolidators are buying recurring revenue while the strongest providers earn record margins; Evergreen Services Group, The 20 MSP, Integris, Thrive, CompassMSP, and Magna5 are active, with ePlus and Cognizant among public buyers. Listed references are CDW, Insight Enterprises, PC Connection, Kyndryl, DXC Technology, Accenture, Cognizant, and EPAM Systems.
- **Cybersecurity.** Losses and staffing shortages are moving security budgets to outside providers, with LevelBlue, CyberMaxx, A-LIGN, and Accenture buying in services and Palo Alto Networks, Google, and ServiceNow in software. Listed references are CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks, Fortinet, Zscaler, Okta, Tenable, Qualys, and Check Point.
- **Telecom and UCaaS.** Infrastructure investors are buying fiber while cloud voice grows about 5% a year: Verizon, AT&T, and T-Mobile are active with KKR and EQT in fiber, and Point Broadband, Ezee Fiber, Vero Fiber, Rightfiber, and TDS Telecom regionally. Listed references are RingCentral, Zoom, Five9, Bandwidth, Ooma, Crexendo, Shenandoah Telecommunications, and TDS.
- **Systems Integration.** Data center construction and security upgrades are filling integrator backlogs; Pavion, Convergint, Everon, Securitas Technology, and Knox Lane buy in security, 26North in audiovisual, and Myriad360 and Net at Work in IT. Listed references are Wesco, ScanSource, Climb Global Solutions, TD SYNNEX, Unisys, Napco Security, Computacenter, Bechtle, and ADT.
- **Electronics and Hardware.** Order books are filling faster than factories can ship, drawing Jabil, Sanmina, and Flex among public manufacturers and Transom Capital, Tide Rock, Align Capital Partners, and Arcline among sponsors. Listed references are Jabil, Flex, Celestica, Sanmina, Plexus, Benchmark Electronics, Kimball Electronics, TTM Technologies, and Keysight.
- **Government Technology.** Budgets for cyber, AI, and mission software are rising while award timing and procurement rules shift; Leidos, Booz Allen, Parsons, and SAIC buy alongside sponsor-backed ERT, Xpect Solutions, B&A, and Detroit Defense. Listed references are Booz Allen Hamilton, CACI, SAIC, Leidos, Parsons, ICF, and Tyler Technologies.

## 5. Deals and Buyers

### Recent Transactions

Ten of the twelve transactions carry a disclosed price, six carry a value to revenue multiple, and two carry a multiple of earnings.

Twelve transactions, two in each sector, show strategic acquirers paying for scarce capability in AI infrastructure services, industrial and data security, counter-drone systems, precision optics, and defense mission software. Stated value to revenue ranges from about 1.0x for a communications software provider to about 20.1x of recurring revenue for an industrial security group, and the two earnings multiples are 9.1x and 10.3x.

Public acquirers disclosed prices for the larger deals, while sponsor-backed platforms and regional operators kept terms private.

- **Managed IT and Cloud.** Cognizant bought Astreya, a San Jose managed services provider with about 2,600 professionals serving six of the largest hyperscalers, for about $600M at announcement ($634M stated at close), about 1.1x revenue as reported by EY. Softcat, a UK reseller, agreed to acquire General Datatech from H.I.G. Capital at a $1.05B enterprise value (H.I.G. Capital).
- **Cybersecurity.** Accenture completed a majority investment in Dragos and the purchase of runZero and NetRise at a combined enterprise value of about $4.18B, about 20.1x their $208 million of annual recurring revenue, which was up 53% (SecurityWeek). Proofpoint, owned by Thoma Bravo, agreed to acquire Varonis for about $6.3B in cash, 8.4x trailing revenue and a 52% premium (Proofpoint).
- **Telecom and UCaaS.** TDS Telecom completed the purchase of Granite State Communications, a New Hampshire fiber and voice provider with more than 11,000 fiber service addresses next to its footprint, on undisclosed terms. BRC Group Holdings agreed to acquire Sangoma, with more than 100,000 business customers and about $200 million of trailing revenue, at about $204M enterprise value, about 1.0x trailing revenue (Business Wire).
- **Systems Integration.** Pavion, backed by Wind Point Partners, acquired Communication Company, a South Bend, Indiana life safety, security, audiovisual, and communications integrator founded in 1976, without disclosing price. Persistent Systems, which held 21%, offered EUR 81 per share for Nagarro, a 140% premium, at EUR 1.27B enterprise value, 1.27x trailing revenue and 9.1x trailing EBITDA (Angel One).
- **Electronics and Hardware.** Motorola Solutions paid $1.5B for D-Fend, an Israeli counter-drone company that expected about $185 million of 2026 revenue, about 8.1x (Motorola Solutions). TTM Technologies agreed to buy the Ophir Optics business from MKS for about $650M in cash, 10.3x expected FY2027 adjusted EBITDA as stated by TTM; MKS states more than 18x on FY2026.
- **Government Technology.** Booz Allen Hamilton paid $720M for Ultra I&C Mission Solutions, an Austin, Texas defense software, encryption, and edge computing business with about 220 employees and EBITDA margins stated as well above 20% (Booz Allen). Leonardo DRS agreed to acquire Raft, a McLean, Virginia AI data fusion firm founded in 2018, for $450M in cash (Leonardo DRS).

### Who Is Buying

Owners of technology businesses draw bids from six types of buyer, and Benchmark International clients were acquired by five of them.

Six types of buyer compete for technology businesses: public and large private strategics, managed service consolidators, sponsor-backed security and integration platforms, private equity firms and family offices, fiber operators and infrastructure investors, and foreign strategics and operating companies.

The eleven Benchmark International clients named in Our Transactions were acquired by one public strategic, three sponsor-backed platforms, four private equity and investment firms, one foreign strategic, and two operating companies.

- **Public and Private Strategics.** Accenture, Cognizant, Motorola Solutions, Booz Allen Hamilton, Leonardo DRS, TTM Technologies, Palo Alto Networks, and ServiceNow are buying capability, customers, and scarce skills faster than they can build them, paying with cash from operations. Cemtrex acquired a Benchmark International client.
- **Managed Service Consolidators.** Evergreen Services Group, Integris, Thrive, CompassMSP, Magna5, and Myriad360 add managed IT providers with contracted monthly revenue and underwrite the recurring base. TRM, backed by 424 Capital, acquired a Benchmark International client.
- **Security and Integration Platforms.** Pavion, Convergint, Everon, Securitas Technology, AVI-SPL, 26North, and Sciens Building Solutions buy regional firms for tenure, local service density, and technician headcount. Zeus Fire and Security, backed by Access Holdings, and AI Fire, backed by Blackstone, acquired Benchmark International clients.
- **Private Equity and Family Offices.** Thoma Bravo, KKR, EQT, Knox Lane, Transom Capital Group, Tide Rock, Align Capital Partners, and Arcline buy founder-owned technology businesses directly, with debt that is lower than a year ago. BlueRock Global, Walnut Capital Partners, and Balius Partners acquired Benchmark International clients.
- **Fiber and Infrastructure Investors.** Incumbents and infrastructure funds, including TDS, Verizon, AT&T, Zayo, Stonepeak, Point Broadband, Ezee Fiber, Vero Fiber, and Rightfiber, are buying local fiber networks and the customers attached to them.
- **Foreign Strategics and Operators.** Softcat, Persistent Systems, Sophos, and Barracuda are entering the United States by acquisition or adding to an existing business. Assessimus, CapCon Networks, and Amtech Drives acquired Benchmark International clients, the latter two operating companies with no sponsor identified.

## 6. Valuations and Public Markets

### Public Companies: Market Performance

Share prices rose a median 54% at security companies and 42% at electronics manufacturers, while government contractors fell 23%.

Over the past 52 weeks, equity markets rewarded the listed peers closest to security spending and the hardware build-out and marked down government contractors, while the median enterprise value to trailing EBITDA multiple edged up to 12.9x from 12.6x a year ago (StockAnalysis).

Of the 51 listed technology companies tracked, 33 trade above their share price of a year ago, and 4 of 6 peer groups carry a higher median EBITDA multiple than a year earlier.

- **Share Price Leaders.** Cybersecurity shares rose a median 54.3% and Electronics and Hardware shares 41.7%, followed by Telecom and UCaaS at 21.7%, on share prices dated October 12 to 19, 2026, against a year earlier (StockAnalysis).
- **Lagging Peer Groups.** Government Technology shares fell a median 22.9% and Systems Integration shares slipped 1.5%, while Managed IT and Cloud gained only 1.1% over the same 52 weeks.
- **EBITDA Growth.** Telecom and UCaaS led median EBITDA growth in the latest fiscal year at 36.0%, ahead of Cybersecurity at 15.8% and Electronics and Hardware at 9.9%; Systems Integration grew 7.0%, Managed IT and Cloud 2.1%, and Government Technology declined 0.3%.
- **Prices Versus Earnings.** Share prices outran earnings in Cybersecurity and Electronics and Hardware, where gains of 54.3% and 41.7% compare with EBITDA growth of 15.8% and 9.9%, while Telecom and UCaaS shares rose 21.7% against 36.0% EBITDA growth.
- **Basis of the Medians.** EBITDA is as reported and includes stock compensation expense, Cybersecurity medians rest on three companies each, and Telecom EBITDA growth excludes Five9 and Ooma, which were set against very small bases.

### Revenue Growth

Electronics manufacturers are forecast to grow 24.6% this year after two years of decline, and security company growth has held at 13% to 19%.

Median revenue growth across the six listed peer groups has diverged sharply. Electronics manufacturers moved from declines of 1.6% and 5.9% in the two earlier years to 8.0% growth in the latest fiscal year and a 24.6% consensus estimate for the current year, while government technology slowed from 10.3% two years ago to a 2.9% decline and a 1.7% estimate (StockAnalysis).

Growth includes acquisitions, and fiscal years end at different dates.

- **Managed IT and Cloud.** Median growth recovered from declines of 5.3% two years ago and 1.8% in the prior year to 6.5% in the latest fiscal year, and consensus expects 4.4% for the current year, with the interquartile range between 1% and 6%.
- **Cybersecurity.** Growth has held in a narrow band, easing from 18.5% two years ago to 13.8% and 13.0% before a 13.9% estimate for the current year, with the 75th percentile company still expected to grow 20%.
- **Telecom and UCaaS.** Median growth slowed from 8.8% and 9.0% in the two earlier years to 4.8% in the latest fiscal year, with 5.0% expected for the current year, although the 75th percentile estimate rises to 20%.
- **Systems Integration.** After a 0.4% decline in the prior year, median growth returned to 6.9% in the latest fiscal year and is estimated at 10.4% for the current year, with the 25th percentile company at 8%.
- **Electronics and Hardware.** The current-year consensus of 24.6% follows two years of decline, and the estimate range runs from 21% at the 25th percentile to 52% at the 75th, the widest spread of the six groups.
- **Government Technology.** Median growth fell from 10.3% two years ago and 9.5% in the prior year to a 2.9% decline in the latest fiscal year, and consensus expects a modest 1.7% recovery in the current year.

### Public Companies: Valuation Metrics

Median EV to EBITDA is higher than a year ago at four of six peer groups, led by electronics manufacturers at 17.4x against 12.1x.

Median enterprise value to EBITDA multiples rose over the year for Managed IT and Cloud, Cybersecurity, Systems Integration, and Electronics and Hardware, and fell for Telecom and UCaaS and Government Technology (StockAnalysis). Cybersecurity carries the highest median on every measure, while Managed IT and Cloud and Systems Integration trade at 0.9x and 0.7x revenue.

Medians can move with one or two companies in a group of nine or fewer, and the Cybersecurity EBITDA multiple rests on three of eight companies.

- **Value to Revenue.** Cybersecurity trades at a median 9.9x trailing revenue, well ahead of Telecom and UCaaS at 2.2x, Electronics and Hardware at 1.3x, Government Technology at 1.2x, Managed IT and Cloud at 0.9x, and Systems Integration at 0.7x.
- **EBITDA Re-Rating.** Electronics and Hardware re-rated most, to 17.4x from 12.1x, while Cybersecurity rose to 22.0x from 20.9x, Systems Integration to 10.1x from 9.1x, and Managed IT and Cloud to 9.7x from 9.3x.
- **Lower Multiples.** Government Technology fell to 10.8x from 12.8x and Telecom and UCaaS to 17.1x from 18.1x, the two peer groups whose median EBITDA multiple is lower than a year ago.
- **Forward Earnings.** Median forward price to earnings is 32.4x for Cybersecurity, 17.9x for Electronics and Hardware, 17.7x for Systems Integration, 14.4x for Telecom and UCaaS, 13.0x for Government Technology, and 12.7x for Managed IT and Cloud.
- **Deal Multiples for Reference.** US buyouts closed in 2025 at a median 12.5x EBITDA (PitchBook, StepStone SPI), TTM Technologies is paying 10.3x expected FY2027 adjusted EBITDA for Ophir Optics, Persistent Systems 9.1x trailing EBITDA for Nagarro, and GF Data Middle Market deals across all industries averaged 7.0x in Q2 2026 (Mercer Capital).

### Public Company Metrics: Electronics and Government Technology

Electronics manufacturers rose a median 42% and trade at 17.4x EBITDA, against government contractors that fell 23% and trade at 10.8x.

Electronics manufacturers trade at a median 1.26x revenue and 17.4x EBITDA against 12.1x a year ago, on median trailing revenue growth of 20.6% and a consensus current-year estimate of 24.6%. Government technology contractors trade at 1.17x revenue and 10.8x EBITDA, down from 12.8x, with trailing revenue down a median 0.8% and an EBITDA margin of 11.3% against 7.0% for the manufacturers (StockAnalysis).

- **Contract Manufacturers.** Celestica trades at 22.6x EBITDA on 47.3% revenue growth and a 65.9% current-year estimate, Sanmina grew 58.5% including the ZT Systems manufacturing business at 11.6x, Flex re-rated to 17.4x from 12.1x, and Jabil trades at 12.8x.
- **Share Price Leaders.** Keysight rose 106.9% and trades at 34.2x on a 25.3% margin, Plexus rose 90.7% to 22.1x from 13.4x, Benchmark Electronics rose 76.5% to 17.0x from 10.2x, and TTM Technologies rose 72.3% with a 52.0% revenue estimate.
- **Growing Contractors.** CACI shares rose 19.5% and its multiple rose to 15.7x from 13.8x on 10.9% revenue growth and a 12.6% current-year estimate, while SAIC rose 36.0% and trades at 10.8x despite a 0.8% revenue decline.
- **Contractor Declines.** Booz Allen Hamilton fell 22.9% on a 7.3% revenue decline to 10.0x from 12.6x, Leidos fell 31.8% to 9.1x from 12.8x, and Parsons fell 47.5% to 12.9x from 19.2x.
- **Tyler Technologies.** Tyler Technologies trades at 6.24x revenue and 33.1x EBITDA, down from 51.8x a year ago, after a 29.1% share decline, with 8.2% revenue growth and an 18.9% EBITDA margin.

### Operating Metrics

Electronics manufacturers are growing about 20% a year, government contractors are flat, and security software converts the most revenue to cash.

Growth, margin, and cash conversion differ sharply across the six peer groups. Electronics manufacturers lead median trailing revenue growth at 20.6% but convert only 3.3% of revenue to free cash flow, while security software converts a median 33.8% on 15.0% growth, and government technology revenue declined a median 0.8% (StockAnalysis).

EBITDA is as reported and is lower than the adjusted EBITDA companies present, most visibly for security software, where stock compensation is excluded from adjusted figures.

- **Revenue Growth.** Median trailing revenue growth is 20.6% for Electronics and Hardware, 15.0% for Cybersecurity, 11.4% for Systems Integration, 5.6% for Managed IT and Cloud, and 5.2% for Telecom and UCaaS, against a 0.8% decline for Government Technology.
- **Fastest Growers.** Sanmina grew 58.5%, Computacenter 53.5%, Celestica 47.3%, TTM Technologies 27.6%, Keysight and Crexendo 25.5% each, Zscaler 25.4%, and Palo Alto Networks 24.5%, with growth that includes acquisitions; four of the eight are electronics and hardware companies.
- **EBITDA Margins.** Telecom and UCaaS has the highest median EBITDA margin at 12.6%, followed by Cybersecurity at 12.0% and Government Technology at 11.3%, while Managed IT and Cloud earns 8.8%, Electronics and Hardware 7.0%, and Systems Integration 5.2%.
- **Free Cash Flow.** Cybersecurity converts a median 33.8% of revenue to free cash flow, far ahead of Government Technology at 10.1% and Telecom and UCaaS at 9.9%; Managed IT and Cloud converts 4.6%, Systems Integration 3.5%, and Electronics and Hardware 3.3%.

### What Public Companies Said

Data center suppliers grew fastest, security vendors tied their outlook to AI adoption, and government contractors split on civilian exposure.

Earnings releases for quarters ended June to August 2026 show the AI infrastructure build-out flowing first to distributors and contract manufacturers, security vendors tying their outlook to AI adoption, and government contractors separating by contract mix. Revenue growth ranged from 62% at Celestica to a 4.2% decline at Booz Allen Hamilton, as stated in each company's release.

- **Data Center Suppliers.** Celestica grew revenue 62%, TD SYNNEX 37.7%, Jabil 28.6%, and Flex 21%, and Wesco grew 13%, which its CEO John Engel described as four consecutive quarters of double-digit sales growth fueled by data centers.
- **Infrastructure Modernization.** CDW grew 10%, with Christine A. Leahy citing customer investment in infrastructure modernization, cloud, and AI-enabled technologies, and Insight Enterprises grew 15%. Jabil's Mike Dastoor described accelerating AI demand complemented by automotive, healthcare, energy infrastructure, defense and aerospace, and automation.
- **Security Vendors.** Palo Alto Networks grew 34%, reflecting the CyberArk acquisition, and CrowdStrike and Fortinet each grew 26%. George Kurtz of CrowdStrike called securing AI the largest market opportunity in the company's history, and Nikesh Arora tied the outlook to a $20 billion FY30 NGS ARR target.

- **Government and Public Sector.** CACI grew 17.6% on a defense-weighted book while civilian-heavy Booz Allen fell 4.2%, and CACI reported $10 billion in contract awards and a 12.3% EBITDA margin for its fiscal year. Tyler Technologies grew 8.2% on record SaaS and total bookings, and Leidos grew 7%.

## 7. Indicators to Watch

Component and copper prices turned up in 2026, data center construction is surging, and IT services employment keeps drifting lower.

Input costs for technology manufacturers and integrators turned sharply higher in 2026: the producer price index for semiconductors and electronic components, flat for four years, rose 26.7% in the year to August, and copper rose 38.6% in the year to July. Demand indicators remain strong in hardware and data center construction, while employment in IT services keeps easing and the 10-year Treasury yield averaged 4.99% in September.

- **Component Prices.** Semiconductor and electronic component prices have risen sharply since February 2026 and are up 26.7% in one year, 32.3% over three years, and 37.7% since September 2021, raising input costs for manufacturers and integrators (Bureau of Labor Statistics via FRED).
- **Copper.** Copper is up 38.6% in one year, 59.8% over three years, and 45.2% since September 2021, reaching a record monthly average in 2026 and raising cabling, power, and fiber construction budgets (International Monetary Fund via FRED).
- **Data Center Construction.** Private office construction, which includes data centers, is up 29.8% in a year and 73.1% since September 2021, and data centers accounted for $85.0 billion of the August 2026 annual rate against $49.1 billion a year earlier (US Census Bureau).
- **IT Services Employment.** Employment in computer systems design and related services is down 1.3% in a year and 4.1% over three years, and headcount has fallen 5% from its March 2023 peak even as technology spending has grown (Bureau of Labor Statistics via FRED).
- **Hardware Orders and Output.** New orders for computers and electronic products are up 16.5% over the year but flat from July to August (US Census Bureau via FRED), while US output of semiconductors and electronic components is up 19.9% in a year and 68.2% since September 2021 (Federal Reserve via FRED).
- **Hosting Prices and Yields.** Hosting and data processing prices are up 2.8% over the year, showing steady but modest pricing power for providers with contracted revenue, while the 10-year Treasury yield is up 0.87 percentage points in a year (Federal Reserve via FRED).

## 8. What We're Reading

Primary sources and trade reporting from the quarter just ended.

Four primary sources from the quarter just ended frame the report's main themes: the scale of AI infrastructure spending, the gap between electronics bookings and shipments, private equity demand for recurring managed services revenue, and the pause in third-party CMMC certification for defense suppliers.

- **Gartner IT Spending Forecast.** Gartner (July 27, 2026) forecasts data center systems spending to rise 62.5% to $822 billion while IT services rise 5.3% to $1.57 trillion, so service firms tied to the infrastructure build-out are growing faster than the rest of the market.
- **EMS Bookings and Shipments.** The Global Electronics Association (October 1, 2026) reports bookings up 53.5% against shipments up 1.0%; buyers value backlog that converts to shipments, which makes component supply a diligence question.
- **Managed Services Consolidation.** Channel Dive (August 19, 2026) reports that acquisitions of managed service providers rose 73% to 64 in the first quarter, 37 of them in North America, and that predictable annual recurring revenue is what investors are paying for.
- **CMMC Certification Pause.** The Department of War Chief Information Officer (July 13, 2026) paused third-party certification for defense suppliers; the task force report reached the CIO on September 11 and is unpublished, and self-assessment against NIST 800-171 still applies (McGuireWoods analysis).
