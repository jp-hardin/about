### What factors influence the valuation of an industrial business?

Industrial company valuations reflect a combination of financial performance, earnings quality, customer concentration, competitive differentiation, capital intensity, and growth prospects. Buyers evaluate normalized EBITDA, sustainable cash flow, capital expenditure requirements, working capital efficiency, manufacturing capabilities, management depth, and end-market exposure.

The relative importance of these factors varies by subsector and business model. Strategic acquisition premiums may also reflect identifiable operating synergies or the scarcity of differentiated capabilities.

### Who are the primary acquirers of middle-market industrial companies?

Acquirers typically include strategic industry participants, private equity-backed operating companies, and qualified financial investors pursuing controlling ownership positions.

Strategic buyers may prioritize complementary manufacturing capabilities, geographic expansion, customer relationships, and operational synergies. Financial buyers generally evaluate earnings durability, management quality, growth opportunities, capital efficiency, and opportunities for operational improvement or acquisition-led expansion.

### How should an industrial business owner prepare for a sale?

Owners should evaluate normalized financial performance, customer and program concentration, equipment condition, maintenance capital expenditures, capacity utilization, technical certifications, management succession, and working capital requirements.

Early transaction planning provides an opportunity to address diligence concerns, institutionalize financial reporting, strengthen operational performance, and demonstrate the sustainability of earnings and cash flow.

### Can an owner retain an equity interest following a sale?

Certain qualifying controlling ownership transactions allow selling shareholders to retain a minority equity interest following closing.

Retained ownership arrangements depend on the buyer's objectives, the transaction structure, shareholder preferences, and applicable regulatory requirements. Such structures can provide continued participation in the company's future performance while allowing owners to realize a portion of their investment.

## Industrial M&A Market Perspectives

### 01 | Supply Chain Resilience & Domestic Manufacturing

Supply chain diversification, reshoring initiatives, and heightened emphasis on domestic production capabilities are influencing strategic acquisition priorities. Businesses with specialized manufacturing capacity, technical qualifications, established OEM relationships, and high switching costs may offer attractive acquisition opportunities for buyers seeking greater supply chain control and operational resilience.

### 02 | Private Equity Consolidation & Platform Expansion

Fragmented industrial markets continue to present opportunities for private equity-backed consolidation strategies. Financial sponsors evaluate acquisitions that expand geographic coverage, introduce complementary capabilities, diversify end-market exposure, or create procurement and operating efficiencies. Businesses with institutionalized operations, strong management teams, and scalable infrastructure are particularly relevant to platform development strategies.

### 03 | Infrastructure Investment, Electrification & Industrial Modernization

Investment in electrical infrastructure, power generation, grid modernization, water systems, and industrial automation creates opportunities across specialized industrial markets. Acquirers assess businesses based on their exposure to durable investment programs, technical capabilities, project execution history, and ability to convert demand into sustainable earnings and cash flow.

### 04 | Recurring Service Revenue & Aftermarket Economics

Industrial services businesses with meaningful maintenance, repair, replacement, and aftermarket revenue can offer attractive earnings characteristics. Buyers evaluate contracted revenue, installed-base exposure, service frequency, customer retention, and the balance between recurring demand and cyclical capital expenditure activity.

### 05 | Manufacturing Productivity & Operating Leverage

Automation, labor availability, production efficiency, and capital investment requirements remain central considerations in industrial acquisitions. Buyers increasingly differentiate businesses based on throughput, capacity utilization, process repeatability, margin performance, and the ability to scale production without disproportionate incremental investment.
